**Binance Pool, the exchange's bitcoin mining pool, found only 1 of roughly 925 bitcoin blocks in the past seven days, about 0.11% of the network, ahead of its shutdown on 10 November, according to CoinDesk.**

Binance announced the closure on 13 October. Mining stops at 07:00 UTC on Tuesday 10 November, when Binance stops accepting hashrate, CryptoSlate reports. Cloud mining, Pool Savings and the transaction accelerator close at the same time. Users have until 23:59 UTC that day to complete any account steps needed for a final payout, and records stay viewable until 24 November.

The pool's last identified block, number 974,743, was mined on 6 November, CoinDesk reports. By its estimate, the pool's computing power fell about 65% from around 26 EH/s in December 2025 to about 9.2 EH/s in October. Miners don't need to switch off their machines: they can point them at another pool. CoinDesk notes that the biggest pools, Foundry USA, AntPool and F2Pool, may pick up most of that hashrate.

For most readers this changes nothing: bitcoin itself keeps running as normal. If you used Binance's cloud mining or Pool Savings, check your balances and payout status before the deadlines.
