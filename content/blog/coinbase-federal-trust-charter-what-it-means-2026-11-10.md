**Short version:** The US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) has given final approval to Coinbase National Trust Company, Coinbase announced on Monday. The federal charter covers custody of digital assets for mainly institutional clients and some related payment services. It does not let Coinbase take ordinary deposits, make loans or issue stablecoins, according to CoinDesk and crypto.news.

## What happened

Coinbase received conditional approval from the OCC, the US federal regulator for national banks, in April 2026. On Monday it said that approval has now become a full national trust charter. crypto.news reports the charter was completed on 6 November, ending an application process that began in October 2025.

Until now, Coinbase's custody business for institutions sat in Coinbase Custody Trust Company, a trust company regulated by New York state since 2018, Decrypt reports. With the new charter, that business moves under a single federal regulator. According to crypto.news, Coinbase plans to move its existing custody business into the federal trust bank over roughly three years.

Coinbase said it spent several months building compliance systems, hiring staff and going through regulatory checks before the OCC signed off, CoinDesk reports. Vice chairman Ryan VanGrack called the approval "another major step" in bringing digital assets into US financial infrastructure.

## What the charter allows, and what it does not

According to crypto.news, the trust bank can:

- hold digital assets in custody as a fiduciary, mainly for institutions;
- hold and transfer assets in custody accounts, plus certain related transaction services;
- connect custody clients to staking, prime trading and financing, which other Coinbase companies provide, not the bank itself.

It cannot take ordinary retail deposits, make traditional loans or issue stablecoins directly, and Coinbase says it has no plans for fractional-reserve banking. The charter also does not give automatic access to Federal Reserve payment systems; that needs a separate Fed review, crypto.news notes. The OCC's April conditions included at least $60 million in Tier 1 capital and enough liquid assets to cover 180 days of operating costs during the first three years.

## Why it matters

Coinbase joins a growing group. Ripple received a full trust charter in October, and Decrypt reports that Circle won final approval in July for a trust bank that will manage USDC reserves. A trust company linked to World Liberty Financial, the Trump family-backed crypto firm, received conditional approval in August, and applications from firms including BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets, Paxos and Kraken's parent Payward are pending, according to crypto.news and Decrypt.

CoinDesk links the trend to the GENIUS Act, the US stablecoin law, which created a route for federal trust companies to issue compliant stablecoins. Not everyone agrees with it. The Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC in October, arguing the agency overstepped its authority by granting trust charters to crypto firms, and Senator Elizabeth Warren argues the approvals are illegal, Decrypt reports.

## What this means for you

This is a US decision about how one company holds assets for mostly institutional clients. It does not change how Coinbase serves retail customers in the EU, where it operates under a MiCA licence.

- **Custody is about who holds the keys, and under which rules.** A federal charter means one national regulator supervises how Coinbase safeguards institutional clients' assets. It is not deposit insurance: crypto held in custody is not covered like a bank account.
- **Indirect effects are possible.** Several US bitcoin and ether ETFs use Coinbase as a custodian, so stronger federal oversight of that business matters to the wider market, including EU investors holding ETPs.
- **Check your own setup.** Whatever exchange you use, know whether your coins are held for you or lent out, and what happens if the firm fails. Our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-explained) and the School lesson on [exchanges and MiCA](/school/exchanges-and-mica) cover the questions to ask.

## What to watch

- The community bankers' lawsuit against the OCC, which could test whether these charters stand.
- Whether the Federal Reserve grants any crypto trust bank direct access to its payment systems.
- Decisions on the pending applications from BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets, Paxos and Payward.
- How Coinbase moves its New York custody business into the federal trust bank over the next three years.
