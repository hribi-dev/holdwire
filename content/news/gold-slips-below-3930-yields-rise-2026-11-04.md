**Gold fell about $21.60, or 0.55%, to around $3,924 an ounce late on US election day, and silver dropped 2.13% to about $49.45, according to Kitco.**

## What happened

Stronger-than-expected US data pushed bond yields higher, which tends to weigh on gold because it pays no interest. September job openings rose to 7.52 million, above the roughly 7.15 million forecast, and factory orders beat expectations too, Kitco reports. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.95%. The Federal Reserve held rates last week, but two policymakers favoured a rise, which keeps a December hike possible.

Gold barely reacted to the overnight election results. It was roughly flat at about $3,944 as Democrats won control of Congress, CryptoTicker reported, while Bitcoin fell 2.6% over the same stretch.

## Tokenised gold in Hong Kong

Separately, a senior official at Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission said a tokenised gold ETF could be the regulator's next pilot for trading tokenised funds, TokenPost reports. It is under consideration, not approved. Hong Kong has already approved 17 tokenised retail products, including gold products, with about $1.3 billion in combined assets.

## What it means for EU readers

Gold remains sensitive to US interest rates. Friday's US jobs report on 6 November is the next big data point. If you hold tokenised gold, check who stores the metal and whether you can redeem it.
