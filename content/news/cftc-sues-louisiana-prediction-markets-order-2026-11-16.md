**The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) has sued the State of Louisiana in federal court to stop it from enforcing a state court order that bars prediction markets such as Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase and Robinhood from serving people in the state, The Block reports.**

What's new: [last week we reported](/news/louisiana-judge-blocks-prediction-markets-2026-11-14) that a Louisiana judge had temporarily blocked these platforms on 12 November. The next day, Friday 13 November, the CFTC and the United States filed suit in the US District Court for the Eastern District of Louisiana. They are asking the court to declare that state gambling laws do not apply to markets the CFTC regulates, and to bar Louisiana from enforcing or extending the state order, according to The Block. The defendants include the state, Attorney General Liz Murrill and the Louisiana Gaming Control Board.

CFTC chair Michael Selig called the state's case "naked lawfare". On Saturday, Murrill replied on X that he had made "flatly false statements" and argued that states have regulated gambling for 250 years, The Block reports. The CFTC says Louisiana is the 12th state it has sued over who controls these markets.

The state order runs until 22 November unless extended, with a hearing on a longer ban set for 30 November.

For EU readers: none of these US platforms is licensed for event contracts in the EU. The case is a test of whether prediction markets in the US are treated as financial products, overseen by the CFTC, or as gambling, overseen by each state.
