**Short version:** The New York Stock Exchange has chosen Avalanche's blockchain technology as the ledger for clearing and settling trades on a planned platform for tokenized stocks, Ava Labs announced on Monday. The venue aims to offer trading around the clock with near-instant settlement, but it still needs regulatory approval and has no confirmed launch date. Avalanche's token, AVAX, rose about 8% over 24 hours, according to CoinDesk.

## What happened

According to CoinDesk, Avalanche will provide the ledger for the clearing firm behind an upcoming NYSE-affiliated alternative trading system (ATS), a type of regulated US trading venue. The NYSE will not settle trades on Avalanche's public network. It will run its own custom chain built with Avalanche's technology, so it can control who takes part.

The plan, as CoinDesk describes it, pairs the NYSE's existing Pillar trading engine with blockchain-based clearing, settlement and custody. The goals are 24/7 trading, instant settlement and funding with stablecoins. Stocks that were tokenized on other blockchains could also be traded, with the Avalanche-based chain recording and settling the trades.

Dennis Lee, the NYSE's head of digital issuance, said the exchange needed a ledger that is fast, keeps costs predictable and lets it control participation, Bitcoin.com News reports. Ava Labs CEO Emin Gün Sirer said the system will support 24/7 "atomic settlement", meaning both sides of a trade change hands at the same moment.

The choice was not sudden. Ava Labs told The Block in September that the NYSE had spent about a year testing Avalanche, and Ava Labs' chief business officer described the project to CoinDesk as "over a year in the making". The Block cites a Wall Street Journal report that the NYSE is aiming for a fourth-quarter launch, while CoinDesk says no date has been given and the round-the-clock trading is "pending regulatory approval".

## Why it matters

Today, buying a share on a US exchange takes a business day to settle, and trading stops in the evening and at weekends. A tokenized share is a digital record of that share on a blockchain. Settling on a shared ledger is meant to make the transfer of the share and the payment happen together, at any hour.

The NYSE is not alone. CoinDesk notes that Nasdaq is building its own tokenized stock infrastructure, including a partnership with Kraken's parent company, and that the DTCC, which settles most US trades, launched a tokenization service last month on a private blockchain and the Canton Network. Last week the NYSE also widened its group of transfer agents (the firms that keep shareholder records) to six, including Securitize and tZERO.

For the crypto market, the notable part is that a public-chain technology won a role at the world's largest stock exchange. Markets reacted: AVAX was up about 8% in 24 hours, according to CoinDesk and KuCoin's daily report. It is worth keeping in mind that the NYSE is using its own permissioned chain, so how much activity flows to Avalanche's public network is not clear from the announcement.

## What this means for you

For readers in the EU, nothing changes yet. The platform is a US venue, it needs approval from US regulators, and it will be offered through brokers, not directly to the public.

- **Tokenized stocks you see today are different.** Some crypto apps already sell "stock tokens" in Europe. Those are usually products issued by a third party that track a share price, and your rights depend on that issuer. Read who issues the token and whether you own the share or only a claim on it.
- **24/7 trading cuts both ways.** Being able to trade at any hour also means prices can move sharply when few people are trading, as weekend crypto sell-offs show.
- **A token rising on news is not the same as adoption.** AVAX moved on the headline. Whether the NYSE platform generates lasting use for Avalanche's public network is a separate question.

The EU is working on its own rules here: ESMA has asked for a future framework for tokenized securities and on-chain settlement as part of the MiCA review. Our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-explained) covers what already applies to crypto firms serving EU customers, and the [glossary](/glossary) explains terms like settlement and stablecoin.

## What to watch

- Whether US regulators approve the NYSE-affiliated ATS, and a firm launch date.
- Which brokers sign up, and whether stablecoin funding is part of the first version.
- How Nasdaq's and the DTCC's projects develop alongside the NYSE's.
- Any detail on how the NYSE's custom chain connects to Avalanche's public network.
