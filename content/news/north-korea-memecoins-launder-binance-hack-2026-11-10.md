**The North Korean hackers behind the October Binance theft created at least six memecoins on the NEAR blockchain and used them to pass about 117,000 NEAR, roughly $430,000, through what looked like ordinary trading, blockchain analytics firm Elliptic said on Monday.**

The method is simple, according to Elliptic. The launderers create a token, which cost about 25 NEAR (around $90) each, and pair it with NEAR in a decentralised exchange pool. One set of wallets buys the token with stolen funds; a second set sells it back and withdraws clean NEAR. The money never moves directly between the two groups, so the proceeds look like trading profits. Some of the tokens used a picture of Elon Musk under an "Elon 2028 President" banner as their logo, The Daily Hodl reports; there is no sign Musk had anything to do with them.

The six tokens were launched between 5 and 7 November. Almost all buying and selling came from the launderers' own wallets, none has traded since 8 November, and the creators of four pulled the remaining liquidity that day, Elliptic says. It found the same trick on Tron and a variant on Solana. The amount is small next to the theft, which Binance puts at $6.34 billion: Bitcoin Magazine reports about $2.85 billion of it has been frozen.

For memecoin traders, the lesson is that a brand-new token with heavy volume can be nothing more than a few wallets trading with each other. Anyone who buys in can be left holding a token nobody else wants.
