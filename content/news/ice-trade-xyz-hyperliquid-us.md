**The company that owns the New York Stock Exchange wants a piece of on-chain derivatives.** Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) is taking a minority stake in trade.xyz, the largest builder of markets on Hyperliquid, and plans a US market for perpetual futures built on the same model, subject to approval by the CFTC, the US derivatives regulator.

## What was announced

- **The stake.** ICE is investing in trade.xyz. Investor Stan Druckenmiller is also backing the company.
- **The plan.** A new US venue, with its own exchange and clearing, would offer perpetual futures modelled on trade.xyz's Hyperliquid markets. ICE would run it exclusively.
- **The condition.** Nothing launches without CFTC approval.

trade.xyz says its existing Hyperliquid markets, which list perps on stocks, indices and commodities, have passed $650 billion in volume in just over a year. Those markets keep running for non-US users.

## Why it matters

Until now, Hyperliquid's growth came from traders outside the US. This is the clearest sign yet that the largest traditional exchange operators see on-chain perpetuals as a product worth competing for, not just a crypto niche. HYPE rose sharply on the news.

## What it doesn't mean

- **Hyperliquid isn't opening to US users.** Hyperliquid's own app remains closed to American traders. US access would come through a separate, regulated venue.
- **It isn't approved yet.** The plan depends on the CFTC.
- **Nothing changes for EU users today.** Hyperliquid is still not authorised under MiCA, so EU protections for licensed providers don't apply.
