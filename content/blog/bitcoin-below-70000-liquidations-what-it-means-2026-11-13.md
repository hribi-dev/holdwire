**Short version:** Bitcoin fell below $70,000 early on Friday, 13 November, for the first time since August. A strong dollar, rising bets on a December rate hike in the US and a sell-off in tech shares after Nvidia's chief executive resigned all weighed on prices. More than $1.3 billion of leveraged crypto positions were forcibly closed in a day, most of them bets on rising prices.

## What happened

Bitcoin dropped to about $69,000, its lowest level since 19 August, according to CoinDesk data. crypto.news put the low at about $68,500 on Binance, after the day opened near $71,460. CoinDesk says bitcoin is now down 17% this quarter, and crypto.news notes it is roughly 45% below its October 2025 record of $126,198.

The rest of the market followed. Ether slipped below $2,000 for the first time since August, XRP traded close to $1 and Solana fell below $80, CoinDesk reports.

This was the second leg of the slide. On Thursday bitcoin had already fallen as low as $71,200, a 12-week low, according to The Block. We covered the first drop below $73,000 [on Wednesday](/blog/bitcoin-three-month-low-hot-us-inflation-2026-11-11).

## The forced selling

Much of the speed came from leverage. More than $1.3 billion of crypto futures positions were liquidated in 24 hours, according to Coinglass data cited by CoinDesk, with bitcoin making up $677 million of that. crypto.news, also citing CoinGlass, counted about $1.23 billion across more than 151,000 traders, with over 96% of the bitcoin liquidations hitting long positions (bets that the price would rise).

CoinDesk says almost $500 million was liquidated as bitcoin fell through $70,000. The largest single liquidation was a long bitcoin position of more than $31 million on Hyperliquid, the decentralised derivatives exchange (see our [Hyperliquid hub](/hyperliquid)).

A liquidation happens when a leveraged trader's losses eat through the margin they put up, and the exchange closes the position automatically. Those forced sales push the price lower, which can trigger the next round.

## Why it happened

No single event explains the fall. The outlets point to several pressures at once:

- **A stronger dollar.** The US Dollar Index rose above 103, its highest since April 2025, CoinDesk reports. Bloomberg also cites higher bond yields weighing on crypto.
- **Rate-hike bets.** October's hotter-than-expected US inflation data raised the odds of a half-point Federal Reserve hike in December, according to CoinDesk.
- **Nvidia.** Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive on Thursday, effective immediately, with finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO. Nvidia shares opened down 12%, The Block reports, adding to an AI-led sell-off that has dragged on bitcoin all month.
- **Fund outflows.** US spot bitcoin ETFs saw about $299.8 million of net withdrawals on 11 November, and roughly $1.44 billion over five sessions, according to Farside Investors data cited by crypto.news.
- **Strategy and MSCI.** Traders are also watching MSCI's removal of Strategy from its indexes on 30 November, which could prompt index funds to sell the shares. Market maker Wintermute told The Block it saw "no convincing signs of size stepping in" and had no estimate of the selling. Our [explainer](/blog/msci-drops-strategy-metaplanet-what-it-means-2026-11-12) covers what that means.
- **Geopolitics and oil.** CoinDesk also cites renewed tension in the Middle East, with US crude near $88 a barrel.

## What this means for you

- **Check any leverage first.** This week shows how quickly leveraged positions get closed in a falling market. If you trade futures or perpetuals, know your liquidation price before the market finds it for you.
- **The euro view is a little different.** Prices above are in US dollars. Because the dollar has strengthened against most currencies, the fall measured in euros has been somewhat smaller. Check your own portfolio in the currency you actually spend.
- **A falling price is not a reason to change platforms in a hurry,** but it is a good moment to confirm the exchange you use is authorised under MiCA. Our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-explained) shows how to check.
- **Watch for scams.** Sharp drops bring out "recovery" and "guaranteed bounce" offers. Nobody can promise either.

## What to watch

- **US data and the Fed.** Further inflation figures and comments from Fed officials will shape the December rate decision that markets are now pricing in.
- **ETF flows.** Whether the run of outflows from US spot bitcoin ETFs continues or reverses.
- **30 November.** The date MSCI removes Strategy from its indexes.
- **Nvidia.** Whether the tech sell-off steadies once the company's leadership situation is clearer.
