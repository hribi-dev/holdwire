## The trade-off

Every investment sits somewhere on a line from **low risk, low return** to **high risk, high possible return**:

- A savings account: very safe, small return.
- A broad stock market fund: can fall 30% or more in a bad year, but has grown over long periods historically.
- A single crypto token: can double in a month, and can also fall 90% and never recover.

**Volatility** is how much a price jumps around. Bitcoin is far more volatile than a stock market fund. Small new tokens are more volatile again.

## Losses are harder than they look

This is the most important number in investing:

| Fall | Gain needed to recover |
|---|---|
| −10% | +11% |
| −25% | +33% |
| −50% | +100% |
| −90% | +900% |

The deeper the fall (the **drawdown**), the harder the climb back. That's why protecting against big losses matters more than chasing big wins.

## Don't put all your eggs in one basket

**Diversification** means spreading money across different things that don't all move together, for example different companies, countries and asset types. When one falls, others may hold up.

Owning ten memecoins is not diversification. They tend to rise and crash together.

## Time and compounding

**Compounding** means your gains start earning gains. €1,000 growing 7% a year becomes about €1,967 after 10 years and about €3,870 after 20, without adding anything. Time in the market does a lot of the work, which is why money for investing should be money you can leave alone.

## The golden rule

Only invest money you can afford to see fall, sometimes a lot, without it changing your life. If a 50% drop would keep you up at night, you're taking too much risk.
