**MiCA is the EU's rulebook for crypto companies.** Since 1 July 2026, any company offering crypto services to people in the EU, such as exchanges, brokers and custodians, must be authorised under it. For you, that means one simple check before you sign up anywhere: is this company in ESMA's MiCA register?

## What MiCA covers

MiCA, the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (EU 2023/1114), does two main things:

1. **It licenses crypto-asset service providers (CASPs).** Exchanges, brokers, custodians and similar firms need authorisation from a national regulator. Once authorised in one EU country, they can serve customers across the EU (this is called passporting).
2. **It sets rules for issuers of stablecoins and other tokens.** Euro- or dollar-backed stablecoins (e-money tokens) and asset-referenced tokens must meet reserve, disclosure and authorisation rules.

It does **not** regulate Bitcoin or Ethereum themselves, since they have no issuer. It regulates the businesses around them.

## What changed on 1 July 2026

MiCA's rules for service providers applied from 30 December 2024. Firms already operating under older national rules got a transition period, which differed by country but could last at most 18 months. That window closed everywhere on **1 July 2026**. Since then, an unauthorised firm serving EU customers is in breach of EU law.

ESMA, the EU markets authority, told unauthorised firms to have wind-down plans in place, and warned customers to move their assets to an authorised provider or a wallet they control.

## What an authorised provider must do

- **Keep your assets separate** from its own, so they aren't lost if the firm goes bust.
- **Meet capital and governance requirements.**
- **Be clear about fees, risks and conflicts of interest.**
- **Have a complaints process.**
- **Follow EU anti-money-laundering rules**, which is why you're asked for ID.

## What MiCA does not do

- It doesn't protect you from **price falls**. Crypto can still lose most of its value.
- It isn't a **deposit guarantee**. Crypto held with a provider is not covered like bank deposits up to €100,000.
- It doesn't cover **decentralised platforms** with no company behind them in the same way. Using one means you don't get MiCA's protections.
- It doesn't regulate **crypto lending and borrowing** as a service.

## How to check a provider in two minutes

1. Find the **legal entity name** in the provider's terms or website footer (for example "Example Exchange Europe Ltd", not just the brand).
2. Open **ESMA's interim MiCA register** and download the CASP list.
3. Search for that entity. Check **which services** it is authorised for (custody, exchange, transfers), and which country authorised it.
4. If it isn't there, assume it isn't authorised. Also check ESMA's list of **non-compliant entities**.

## FAQ

**Is my exchange safe if it's MiCA-authorised?**
Safer, not safe. Authorisation means rules on custody, capital and conduct, plus a regulator watching. It doesn't remove price risk or guarantee your assets.

**Can I still use an unauthorised or decentralised platform?**
Using one isn't illegal for you as a customer in most cases, but you get none of MiCA's protections. Check your country's rules.

**Does MiCA apply outside the EU?**
It applies to firms serving people in the EU, wherever the firm is based. The countries of the European Economic Area apply it too.
