**Short version:** The crypto sell-off sped up over the weekend. Bitcoin briefly touched $62,000 late on Saturday, 14 November, its lowest level in about three months, and ether fell more than 10%. More than $2 billion of leveraged positions were forcibly closed in 24 hours. The same pressures as earlier in the week are behind it: bets on a US rate hike in December, high bond yields and money leaving bitcoin funds.

## What happened

Two days ago we wrote about bitcoin [falling below $70,000](/blog/bitcoin-below-70000-liquidations-what-it-means-2026-11-13). The slide did not stop there.

On Saturday morning, bitcoin broke below Friday's low of about $67,555 and traded near $65,000 by 10 a.m. New York time, according to Bitcoin Magazine. Selling picked up again in the evening. Bitcoin fell through $64,000, and by 23:03 UTC it was at about $63,528, down 6.6% in 24 hours, according to CoinGecko data cited by CryptoTicker. TokenPost reports a brief low of $62,000 a few hours later.

The rest of the market fell harder. CryptoTicker's figures at 23:03 UTC:

- **Ether:** about $1,745, down 9.1%
- **XRP:** about $0.898, down 10.7%
- **Solana:** about $69.19, down 10.4%
- **Dogecoin:** about $0.056, down 12.0%
- **Total crypto market value:** down 9.2% to about $2.16 trillion

Bitcoin is now about 21% below its 5 November close of $80,443, CryptoTicker calculates, and roughly half its October 2025 record of $126,080. In euros it touched about €60,600, the lowest since August. The Fear and Greed Index, a sentiment gauge, fell to 13 out of 100, "extreme fear".

## The forced selling

More than 320,000 traders were liquidated in 24 hours, for a total above $2 billion, TokenPost reports. In bitcoin alone, about $645 million of long positions (bets on rising prices) were closed, against $62 million of shorts. Ether longs lost about $432 million.

The worst of it came fast. In the hour after bitcoin broke $64,000, $691 million of positions were liquidated, about 97% of them longs, TokenPost reported.

A liquidation is when an exchange automatically closes a leveraged position because the trader's losses have used up their margin. Those forced sales push the price down further, which can trigger the next wave. Weekend markets make this worse: order books are thinner, and spot bitcoin ETFs do not trade, CryptoTicker notes.

## Why it happened

The outlets point to the same mix of pressures as earlier in the week, only stronger:

- **Rate-hike bets.** Futures markets now fully price a quarter-point rise in US interest rates at the Federal Reserve's 8–9 December meeting, and largely price another one in January, TokenPost reports. A week ago, the market-implied chance of a January hike was about 10%. Two Fed officials, Susan Collins and Mary Daly, signalled on Friday that more tightening may be needed, Bitcoin Magazine reports.
- **Bond yields and the dollar.** The 10-year US Treasury yield rose as high as 5.17% on Friday, and the dollar index hit a 19-month high, according to Bitcoin Magazine. Higher safe returns make risky assets less attractive.
- **ETF outflows.** US spot bitcoin ETFs had seven straight days of net outflows through 13 November, about $1.91 billion in total, TokenPost reports. Spot ether ETFs have now had outflows for seven weeks in a row.
- **Tech shares.** Chip stocks fell again on Friday, extending the sell-off that followed Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia's chief executive, Bitcoin Magazine notes.

Onchain analyst James Check, quoted by TokenPost, called it the fourth "capitulation-style" sell-off since bitcoin's record. He said most of the selling came from recent buyers who paid more than $80,000.

## What this means for you

- **Know your liquidation price.** If you use leverage, check where your position gets closed, and remember that a weekend gap can reach it before you can react. Isolated margin limits a loss to one position; cross margin puts your whole balance at risk.
- **Check crypto loans.** If you borrowed against bitcoin or ether, look up your provider's top-up threshold and how long you have to respond. Some platforms sell collateral automatically, weekends included.
- **Expect the ETF reopening to matter.** US spot bitcoin and ether ETFs reopen on Monday at 15:30 CET. Their flows have been a big part of this month's selling.
- **Watch for scams.** Sharp falls bring "recovery" offers and fake support accounts. Our [scam-spotting lesson](/school/spotting-scams) covers the common tricks.
- **Don't treat any level as a floor.** Analysts quote support levels, but none of them is a promise. Nobody knows where the bottom is.

## What to watch

- **Monday's ETF flows**, the first chance for US funds to react to the weekend fall.
- **Nvidia's results on Tuesday**, its first report under interim CEO Colette Kress, which could move tech shares and, through them, crypto.
- **US data before the 8–9 December Fed meeting**, and any change in rate-hike odds.
- **Stablecoin supply.** The combined supply of USDT, USDC and DAI fell by a record $11.5 billion over 30 days, according to Coinbase Institutional data cited by CryptoTicker. Less stablecoin money usually means less buying power on exchanges.
