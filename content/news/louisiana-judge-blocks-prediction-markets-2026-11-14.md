**A Louisiana state judge has ordered ten prediction market operators, including Kalshi, Polymarket US, Coinbase, Robinhood and Crypto.com, to stop offering event contracts to people in the state for at least ten days, DeFi Rate reports.**

Judge Jeffrey Johnson of the 21st Judicial District Court issued the temporary restraining order on Thursday afternoon, 12 November, the same day Attorney General Liz Murrill sued, according to UPI. He ruled without hearing the companies' arguments. The order covers contracts on sports, elections and awards offered without a state gaming licence, requires the firms to block Louisiana users by location, and bars them from telling customers there that the products are legal. The other operators named are ForecastEx, Gemini, Novig, ProphetX and Underdog.

The order runs until 22 November unless it is extended, and a hearing on a longer injunction is set for 30 November, UPI reports. Kalshi has asked a federal court to stop Louisiana from enforcing its gambling laws against it, according to DeFi Rate.

It is the latest round in a fight over who regulates these markets. Kalshi's head of litigation argues that federally regulated exchanges answer to federal rules alone, UPI reports, while several states treat the contracts as unlicensed betting. CoinDesk reported on Friday that the CFTC, the US derivatives regulator, has itself filed a lawsuit against Arkansas over prediction markets.

The order applies to people in Louisiana only. It does not change anything for customers of these companies in the EU.
