**Bitcoin is digital money that no company or government issues.** It runs on a public network of computers that anyone can join, its supply is capped at 21 million coins, and you can hold it yourself without a bank. In the EU you can buy it through providers authorised under MiCA, then keep it on the platform or move it to your own wallet.

## How Bitcoin works

Bitcoin was described in a 2008 paper by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto, and the network went live in January 2009. Instead of a bank keeping a ledger of who owns what, thousands of computers keep identical copies of a shared ledger called the **blockchain**.

New transactions are grouped into blocks roughly every ten minutes. Computers called **miners** compete to add the next block by solving a costly computational puzzle. This is **proof of work**. The winner earns newly created bitcoin plus the fees users paid. Because rewriting history would mean redoing all that work faster than the rest of the network, confirmed transactions are very hard to reverse.

The reward for mining a block halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. This **halving** is how the supply approaches its 21 million limit.

The smallest unit of bitcoin is a **satoshi**: one hundred-millionth of a bitcoin (0.00000001 BTC). You don't need to buy a whole coin.

## Why people hold it

- **Fixed supply.** No one can print more than 21 million, which is why some people compare it to digital gold.
- **No middleman.** You can send it to anyone, anywhere, at any hour, without asking a bank.
- **Self-custody.** If you hold your own keys, nobody can freeze or seize your coins through a platform.
- **Liquidity.** It is the most traded crypto-asset, with markets open around the clock.

## Bitcoin vs gold

| | Bitcoin | Gold |
|---|---|---|
| Supply | Capped at 21 million | Grows slowly through mining |
| Storage | A wallet you control, or a provider | A vault, a safe or a provider |
| Moving it | Minutes, across borders | Slow and costly to move physically |
| Track record | Since 2009 | Thousands of years |
| Price swings | Very large | Much smaller |

## The risks

- **Volatility.** Bitcoin's price regularly moves 20% or more in weeks. You can lose a large part of what you put in.
- **Self-custody mistakes.** If you lose your seed phrase, your coins are gone. No one can reset it.
- **Scams.** Fake exchanges, "recovery services" and impersonators target new buyers.
- **Platform risk.** Unlicensed platforms can fail or disappear with customer funds.

## How to buy Bitcoin in the EU

1. **Choose an authorised provider.** Since 1 July 2026, only crypto-asset service providers authorised under MiCA may serve EU customers. Check the provider's legal entity in ESMA's register.
2. **Verify your identity.** Expect to upload an ID document. This is required by EU anti-money-laundering rules.
3. **Deposit euros by SEPA**, often the cheapest method.
4. **Buy**, checking the total cost: trading fee plus the spread between buy and sell prices.
5. **Decide where to keep it.** Small amounts can stay with an authorised provider. For larger amounts, many people use a hardware wallet they control.

## FAQ

**Who created Bitcoin?**
A person or group using the name Satoshi Nakamoto, whose identity has never been confirmed.

**Is Bitcoin legal in the EU?**
Yes. Holding and buying Bitcoin is legal. MiCA regulates the companies that provide crypto services, not Bitcoin itself, because Bitcoin has no issuer.

**Can Bitcoin be shut down?**
There is no central company to shut down. Governments can restrict exchanges and banks that deal with it, but the network itself keeps running as long as people run it.

**Do I pay tax on Bitcoin?**
In most EU countries, gains are taxable in some form, but rules differ a lot. Check your country's rules or speak to a tax adviser.
