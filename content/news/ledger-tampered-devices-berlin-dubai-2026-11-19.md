**Ledger said on Wednesday, 18 November, that it has found "a few" tampered hardware wallets in Berlin and is investigating reports of two more bought in Dubai, widening a case that began with a reseller in Southeast Asia.**

Every confirmed device had physical modifications designed to capture the recovery phrase during setup, TokenPost reports. Ledger says the attackers bought genuine devices, fitted extra hardware and put them back into reseller channels. It calls the new cases part of "the same incident" as the Southeast Asian ones, according to The Block. One Dubai report involves the newer Nano Gen5.

Ledger says there is no flaw in its secure chip or software and no confirmed case involving a device bought on ledger.com. It has not confirmed any loss figures. Blockchain data firm Bitquery estimated about $1.68 million taken from 16 wallets in Dubai and Berlin, The Block reports. Ledger asks anyone who bought a device in Berlin since January to contact its support.

What to do if you are unsure about a device, according to Ledger: don't use it to create or restore a wallet. Move funds to a new wallet set up on a device bought directly from the maker, and don't reuse the old recovery phrase. Ledger support can arrange an inspection of suspect devices.

Rival maker Trezor confirmed similar implants in devices from the same Southeast Asian reseller last week.
