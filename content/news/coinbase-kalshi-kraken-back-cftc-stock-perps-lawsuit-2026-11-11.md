**Coinbase, Kalshi, Kraken parent Payward, DRW, Jump Trading and Paradigm asked a US federal court on Monday for permission to file a brief supporting the CFTC in a lawsuit that seeks to overturn its approval of single-stock perpetual futures, The Block reports.**

The lawsuit was filed in federal court in New York on 3 November by the Capital Markets Alliance Association, whose members include Cboe and Citadel Securities, according to Unchained. It argues that perpetual futures on individual US stocks never expire and should count as "security-based swaps", which cannot be listed on CFTC-regulated exchanges, and that the CFTC stepped on the SEC's turf.

The firms have a direct stake. Unchained reports that applications from Kalshi, Coinbase Derivatives and Bitnomial to list such contracts took effect on 2 November, when the CFTC let its 45-day review window lapse without objecting; The Block says Payward applied too. The court has already turned down the alliance's request for a temporary restraining order, Unchained reports. In their filing, the six firms argue that the SEC was involved from the start, that security futures are not swaps under the law Congress set up in 2000, and that equity perps already trade abroad. They warn that a ruling for the alliance would push these products offshore.

For EU readers this is a US fight, but it shapes where these products are offered. Perpetual futures are leveraged and can be wiped out quickly; under EU rules, retail access to leveraged derivatives is restricted.
