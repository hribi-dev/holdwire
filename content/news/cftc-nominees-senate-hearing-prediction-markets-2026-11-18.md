**The Senate Agriculture Committee questioned President Trump's two nominees for the US derivatives regulator on Tuesday, 17 November, and prediction markets dominated the hearing.**

Matthew Rowland, the Democratic pick, and Nathan Anonick, the Republican pick, would join chairman Michael Selig, currently the Commodity Futures Trading Commission's only commissioner, The Block reports. Even if both are confirmed, two of the five seats would stay empty, according to ChainCatcher.

Senator Tina Smith argued that prediction markets are operating in violation of state and tribal laws. Rowland pointed to a 2011 CFTC rule that bars certain event contracts, including those involving gaming, and said he was sympathetic to the concerns of states and tribes, but would not comment on active lawsuits. Anonick said the agency has discretion to decide whether a contract is against the public interest, The Block reports.

The dispute is growing. Under Selig the CFTC claims exclusive authority over event contracts, while nearly a dozen states and the agency have sued each other, and the question could reach the Supreme Court.

Time is short. The committee must vote before the full Senate does, and nominations not confirmed before the new Congress meets in January generally have to be resubmitted.

For EU readers, this is a US fight, but its outcome will shape how platforms such as Kalshi and Polymarket operate and how other regulators view them.
