**Circle, the company behind the USDC stablecoin, reported third-quarter revenue and reserve income of $864.9 million, up 17% on a year earlier and above the roughly $776.7 million analysts expected, according to Bloomingbit.**

## What happened

Net income from continuing operations rose 5% to $226 million, far above the $91.6 million estimate, and adjusted EBITDA rose 85% to $317 million, CoinDesk reports. A large part of the beat was one-off: Circle booked the full $182 million from its ARC token presale in the third quarter, while the market had expected it to be spread over two quarters, Bloomingbit says.

USDC itself grew more slowly. Average circulation rose 8% year on year to $73.3 billion. On-chain USDC volume reached $13.9 trillion, up 49%, according to CoinDesk. Circle kept its full-year guidance. It also laid off fewer than 70 staff in September, about 5% of its workforce, which CEO Jeremy Allaire said was about using AI and unrelated to the crypto market.

## Context

Most of Circle's income comes from interest on the reserves behind USDC, so its revenue depends heavily on US interest rates and on how much USDC is in circulation. The overall stablecoin market recently fell to its lowest level in a year ([our brief](/news/stablecoin-market-cap-lowest-in-a-year-2026-11-03)).

## What it means for EU readers

USDC and Circle's euro stablecoin EURC are among the stablecoins EU exchanges can offer under MiCA. A healthy issuer is good news for holders, but a stablecoin is not a bank deposit and has no deposit insurance.
