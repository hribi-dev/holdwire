**Short version:** Deutsche Bank, Germany's largest lender, made its crypto custody service available to paying clients on Wednesday, Reuters reported, according to PYMNTS and crypto.news. The bank now holds bitcoin, ether and three stablecoins for corporate and institutional customers, managing the private keys itself. It runs under a MiCA authorisation from Germany's BaFin, so it can serve clients across the EU. Retail customers are not included.

## What happened

Deutsche Bank first announced the service on 16 September, saying it would launch once regulators signed off. That approval came on 12 October, when BaFin recorded its permission for crypto custody and transfer services and the bank appeared in ESMA's MiCA register, Cointelegraph and crypto.news report.

At launch the bank supports five assets, according to crypto.news:

- bitcoin (BTC) and ether (ETH)
- Circle's dollar stablecoin USDC and euro stablecoin EURC
- EURAU, a euro stablecoin from AllUnity, a company Deutsche Bank backs

Clients reach the service through the bank's Autobahn corporate platform. The bank says it uses hardware-protected key storage, separate warm and cold wallets, approvals by several people and segregation of client assets. Some technical parts come from outside providers; Deutsche Bank has worked with Swiss crypto infrastructure firm Taurus since 2023, Cointelegraph reports.

The first clients come from the bank's corporate and investment banking side: companies, asset managers, hedge funds, brokers and public institutions, according to the September release. The bank has not named any customers or published fees. Trading, exchange services and tokenized securities are not part of the first version.

On the same day, Deutsche Bank signed a memorandum of understanding with its majority-owned asset manager to explore tokenized money market funds, crypto exchange-traded commodities and stablecoins. No products or dates were announced. Gerald Podobnik, co-head of the corporate bank, told Reuters in October that the bank plans to take custody to Asian financial centres in 2027.

## Why it matters

Custody is the plumbing of institutional crypto. A pension fund or company treasurer often can't hold bitcoin unless a regulated, familiar counterparty keeps the keys. Until now, in Europe that mostly meant specialist crypto firms. A bank of Deutsche Bank's size offering it under MiCA gives large clients a route that their compliance teams already know.

It is also part of a pattern. Paul Maley, the bank's global head of trust and securities services, said the medium-term aim is a "fully integrated, regulated ecosystem" for custody, tokenization and settlement, PYMNTS reports. Deutsche Bank is one of the 21 institutions backing the bank stablecoin venture we covered yesterday ([Project Faro](/blog/banks-270-million-stablecoin-project-faro-what-it-means-2026-11-11)). In the US, BNY and State Street already run digital asset custody, crypto.news notes.

The choice of stablecoins is telling too. Two of the three are euro tokens, and Tether's USDT, the biggest stablecoin worldwide, is not on the list.

## What this means for you

- **You can't use this service as a private customer.** It is for corporate and institutional clients only.
- **It's a signal of where EU crypto is heading.** Big banks are building crypto services under MiCA, starting with their largest clients.
- **If your bank offers crypto, ask who holds the keys.** With a custodian, the bank or its partner holds them; with your own wallet, you do. Our lesson on [wallets and seed phrases](/school/wallets-and-seed-phrases) explains the trade-off.
- **Crypto is not a bank deposit.** Deutsche Bank's own release points out that crypto assets are not covered by a deposit guarantee comparable to the one for bank deposits. That applies to any provider.
- **Check the register.** Any firm serving EU customers should be authorised under MiCA. [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained) and our lesson on [exchanges and MiCA](/school/exchanges-and-mica) show how to check.

## What to watch

- **First named clients and fees**, which will show how much demand there is.
- **New assets.** The bank has said more coins and tokenized financial instruments are on its roadmap.
- **The asset-manager tie-up**, which could lead to crypto ETCs or tokenized funds that retail investors can buy.
- **Other large EU banks** following with their own MiCA custody licences. Our [fintech section](/fintech) tracks what banks and neobanks offer.
