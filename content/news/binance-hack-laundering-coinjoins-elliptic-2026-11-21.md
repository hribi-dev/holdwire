**Launderers linked to North Korea have mixed at least 2,310 BTC, worth about $149 million, from the October Binance hack through coinjoins, about 90% of it since Tuesday, according to blockchain analytics firm Elliptic.**

A coinjoin pools many users' bitcoin into one transaction so it is harder to trace who paid whom. Elliptic's Tom Robinson said the coinjoins were organised by Kruw, a coordinator for the Wasabi Wallet software, The Block reports. Coordinators arrange the mixing but never hold the funds.

The attackers are also moving ether. About 488,000 ETH, valued at $860 million, has moved from five wallets, and about $70 million of it was swapped into bitcoin through THORChain and mixed within hours, according to The Block's report on Elliptic's findings.

Even so, Elliptic believes very little has been cashed out so far. Its tally puts about $2.35 billion in wallets linked to the theft and $2.85 billion frozen. Binance said on 18 November it had won a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts.

For EU users the practical point is that hack proceeds are now spreading through mixers and swaps. Coins that touch them can be flagged by exchanges later, so be careful buying crypto peer-to-peer from strangers.
