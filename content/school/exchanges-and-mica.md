## Where most people start

Most beginners buy crypto through a **centralised exchange** or a broker app. You send euros by bank transfer or card, buy crypto, and either keep it there or move it to your own wallet.

The choice of platform matters more than which coin you pick first.

## Step 1: check it's authorised

The EU's crypto rulebook, **MiCA**, requires any firm offering crypto services to people in the EU to be authorised as a **crypto-asset service provider (CASP)**. The transition period for older national licences ended on **1 July 2026**.

Before you sign up:

1. Find the company's legal name (usually in the website footer or terms).
2. Search for it in the **ESMA interim MiCA register** of authorised CASPs.
3. If you can't find it, don't send it money.

MiCA authorisation isn't a guarantee nothing will go wrong, and it doesn't protect you from price falls. But it means the firm must keep customer assets separate, follow conduct rules and answer to a regulator. Our guide [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained) covers what it does and doesn't protect.

## Step 2: expect ID checks (KYC)

Regulated platforms must **know your customer (KYC)**: they'll ask for ID, sometimes a selfie and proof of address. This is required by anti-money-laundering law.

A platform that lets you trade large amounts with no checks at all is not doing you a favour. It's usually a sign it isn't regulated.

## Step 3: understand the real cost

Fees come in three main forms:

- **Trading fee:** a percentage of each trade.
- **Spread:** the gap between the buy and sell price. "0% fee" apps often earn through a wider spread.
- **Withdrawal and network fees:** charged when you move crypto out or euros back to your bank.

Compare the total cost on a realistic amount, not just the headline fee.

## Step 4: protect the account

Turn on **two-factor authentication** with an authenticator app (not SMS if you can avoid it), use a unique password, and bookmark the real website.
