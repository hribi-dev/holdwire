**Short version:** On 3 November the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) said perpetual futures on individual US stocks can trade on regulated US exchanges for the first time. Within hours, a trade group backed by Cboe and Citadel Securities sued to overturn the decision. The product is the same kind of contract that made [Hyperliquid](/hyperliquid) popular, so the fight over who regulates it matters well beyond Wall Street.

## What happened

A perpetual future, or "perp", is a contract that tracks a price and never expires. Traders can hold it for as long as they keep enough margin. Perps are the main product on crypto exchanges, and Hyperliquid now lists perps on chip and other stocks through HIP-3 markets built by outside teams ([our brief](/news/hyperliquid-chip-stock-perps-dip-buying-2026-11-03)).

Three US exchanges filed to list stock perps on 18 September: Kalshi, Coinbase Derivatives and Bitnomial, the exchange owned by Kraken's parent Payward. The CFTC had 45 days to object. It did not, so the contracts were "deemed approved" when the review window closed on 2 November, according to Cryptonews. CFTC Chairman Michael Selig confirmed this on 3 November.

According to Bankless, the contracts come with limits that crypto traders will notice:

- They count as **security futures**, so both the CFTC and the SEC oversee them. Only exchanges registered with both can list them.
- They trade from Sunday evening to Friday afternoon, US Eastern time, not 24/7.
- Margin floors are around 15%, or roughly 6.5x leverage. Offshore crypto venues allow much more.

Kalshi's filing covers up to 58 large companies, including Apple, Nvidia and Tesla, and Bitnomial's starter list has ten names. Kalshi told MarketWatch it plans to launch "in the coming days", DeFi Rate reports. Coinbase said it intends to list the contracts pending any further approvals. Selig said the aim is to keep US traders away from offshore, often unregulated, venues.

## The lawsuit

The Capital Markets Alliance, whose members include Cboe, Citadel Securities and the Healthy Markets Association, filed suit in the US District Court for the Southern District of New York. Its argument, according to Cryptonews and PANews:

- A contract with no expiry date and no delivery obligation is a **swap**, not a future. Swaps based on single stocks belong to the SEC alone.
- The CFTC broke administrative law by not explaining its decision or weighing the risks to investors.
- The products would pull customers and revenue away from the group's members' stock and options businesses.

The group wants the approvals cancelled and an order stopping trading while the case runs. Cboe's chief executive, Craig Donohue, said these products "should trade on platforms regulated under U.S. securities laws". They are not the only critics: Bankless notes that the industry body SIFMA asked regulators to pause for public comment, and CME is already suing the CFTC over its earlier approval of crypto perps.

## Why it matters for Hyperliquid

Hyperliquid's own stock perps stay off-limits to US users. The new onshore contracts do not change that. But the ruling is still a test for Hyperliquid's US plans. On 29 October, NYSE owner ICE and trade.xyz announced a plan for a US-regulated perps market built on Hyperliquid, including stock markets ([our brief](/news/ice-trade-xyz-hyperliquid-us)). That plan is not part of this lawsuit. However, Cryptonews notes that if a court decides these contracts are swaps, the futures route that plan relies on could become harder.

David Schamis, chief executive of Hyperliquid Strategies, called the CFTC's classification a real step toward bringing Hyperliquid onshore, according to Bankless.

## What this means for you

For EU readers, nothing changes directly: these contracts are for US exchanges and their customers. In the EU, leveraged products on single stocks sold to retail clients fall under MiFID II and ESMA's product rules, not MiCA, and leverage on single shares is capped far below crypto-exchange levels.

The bigger point is that stock perps are moving from crypto's edge into regulated markets. That brings lower leverage and more investor protection, but also a fight between regulators that could take months. If you trade stock perps on a crypto venue, check whether that venue is allowed to serve you where you live, and remember that leverage magnifies losses as well as gains.

## What to watch

- **A court order.** The group asked for a temporary restraining order. If the judge grants it, launches could be frozen within days.
- **Kalshi's first listings.** Whether Kalshi goes live before the court acts.
- **The SEC's response.** The SEC has to accept these products as security futures for the CFTC's approach to hold.
- **ICE and trade.xyz.** Any update on how their Hyperliquid-based plan will be classified. Our [Hyperliquid hub](/hyperliquid) tracks it.
