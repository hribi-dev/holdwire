**The Liquid Federation said on 3 November that its bitcoin reserves are "fully restored", meaning every LBTC token is again backed 1:1 by real bitcoin, about two months after an exploit drained roughly 4,000 BTC (about $320 million).**

## What happened

Liquid is a Bitcoin sidechain run by a group of companies called the Liquid Federation. Users lock bitcoin and receive LBTC, a token meant to be redeemable 1:1. On 6 September, a bug in Elements, the software that runs Liquid, let an attacker create about 4,000 LBTC without depositing bitcoin and redeem them for real BTC, according to Liquid's incident report as reported by The Block. Reserves fell from about 4,205 BTC to 197 BTC, CoinDesk reports.

The attacker returned about 3,400 BTC the next day, leaving a shortfall of roughly 600 BTC. Blockstream refused the attacker's demand for a 10% bounty. Replacement deposits arrived in late October, and two final transfers of 300 BTC and 250 BTC landed on 2 November. Federation board member Samson Mow attributed the funding to Blockstream. The federation has not published a breakdown.

## What it means for you

If you hold LBTC, it is fully backed again, according to the federation. But direct federation peg-outs are still restricted with no confirmed reopening date. Swaps between BTC and LBTC work through providers such as SideSwap, which can queue withdrawals when its own balance runs low, crypto.news reports. The federation says governance changes will follow "in the coming weeks."

A wrapped or bridged coin is only as good as the system behind it. Before using one, check who holds the reserves and how you get your bitcoin back.
