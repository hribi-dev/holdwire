**An attacker drained about 55.2 million KAVA, worth roughly $3.76 million before the attack, from Kava Lend, the lending protocol on the Kava blockchain, late on Friday, 13 November, according to The Block.**

The attacker used Kava Lend's old BUSD market, which had sat almost unused since Paxos stopped issuing BUSD in 2023. The Block's analysis says a tiny loan pushed the market's supply to a fraction of a cent, and a rounding flaw in how the protocol shares out interest then made the attacker's BUSD deposit count as about 1.25 billion BUSD of collateral. The attacker borrowed KAVA against it 127 times, emptying the pool in about an hour and a half. Onchain records suggest preparations started on 8 November.

Most of the stolen KAVA was moved to BNB Chain and sold within an hour for about $2.09 million in BNB and USDT. Kava said the incident was "isolated and contained within Kava Lend" and not an infinite mint, and promised a full report. South Korean exchanges Upbit and Coinone suspended KAVA deposits and withdrawals on Saturday, TokenPost reported, and KAVA traded near $0.042, down about 39% in 24 hours.

For EU readers: the loss is small next to this autumn's big hacks, but the pattern is common. Old, unused markets inside lending protocols are a favourite target. If you lend on a DeFi platform, check which markets are still live and whether the team has retired the ones nobody uses.
