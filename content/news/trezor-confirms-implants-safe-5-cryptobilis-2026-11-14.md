**Hardware wallet maker Trezor has confirmed that three Safe 5 devices bought from CryptoBilis, a former reseller in Southeast Asia, contained hidden implants that copy the recovery words shown on screen, Bitcoin.com News reported on 12 November.**

The implant is an extra circuit board fitted between the screen and the main board. It records the recovery words and any passphrase displayed, encrypts them and sends them out over a mobile network, according to Trezor's update as reported by Bitcoin.com News. It does not change the firmware or the secure chip, which is why the device still looks genuine. Trezor says it works like implants found earlier in some Ledger devices sold by the same reseller.

All three were bought between 20 August and 23 September, directly from CryptoBilis or from its Tokopedia and Shopee shops. Trezor checked devices from 48 customers who got in touch and found no tampering in devices from other resellers or in other models. No owner has reported losing funds. Trezor first warned CryptoBilis buyers on 29 October.

Firmware 2.13.1, released on 11 November, stops the implants found so far from capturing data, but it cannot protect a recovery phrase that was already captured, CoinMarketCap Academy notes. "We have cut our reseller network by about 80%," Trezor said.

If you bought a Trezor from CryptoBilis since March 2026, move your funds to a new wallet with a new recovery phrase and ask Trezor for a replacement. Everyone else: buy hardware wallets from the maker or an official reseller, and never type your recovery phrase into anything when someone asks you to. Trezor warns that scammers are already using the case in phishing emails, letters and calls.
