**Spot gold fell 2.8% to $4,021.36 an ounce by mid-morning in New York on Tuesday after US consumer prices rose faster than expected in October, according to Reuters figures published by MINING.COM. Silver dropped 3.9% to $51.32.**

The US consumer price index rose 0.4% on the month, against a forecast of 0.3%, and core prices rose 0.3% against 0.2%. The dollar and Treasury yields climbed. Markets now fully price a quarter-point Fed rate hike in December, and the chance of a half-point move rose to 10.5% from zero before the data, according to CME FedWatch cited by Reuters. Higher rates make gold, which pays no interest, less attractive to hold.

Gold had already slipped about 1% earlier in the day after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a half-point hike should be considered, Kitco reports. Marex analyst Edward Meir called the inflation surprise a short-term negative for gold. Standard Chartered noted that gold is still holding above $4,000, with oil, inflation and stock markets setting the tone.

The drop reverses most of Monday's gain, when gold rose almost 2% on falling oil prices. For euro-based buyers, a stronger dollar can soften the fall when gold is priced in euros.
