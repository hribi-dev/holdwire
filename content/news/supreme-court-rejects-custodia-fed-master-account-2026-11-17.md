**The US Supreme Court on Monday, 16 November, declined to hear Custodia Bank's case against the Federal Reserve, ending a six-year effort by the crypto-focused Wyoming bank to get direct access to the Fed's payment system.**

A master account lets a bank settle payments directly through the Fed instead of going through a partner bank. Custodia, founded by Caitlin Long, applied to the Kansas City Fed in October 2020 and was turned down in January 2023 over concerns about its crypto business, The Block reports. A district court sided with the Fed in March 2024, and the Tenth Circuit appeals court agreed in October 2025 by 2–1, ruling that Reserve Banks can refuse even eligible applicants, according to Bitcoin Magazine. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case in March.

The Supreme Court's refusal leaves that ruling in place. Custodia had not commented by Monday morning US time. Other crypto firms have taken different routes: Kraken's banking arm became the first crypto company to get a master account in March, and the Fed has consulted on a limited "skinny" account without interest or emergency borrowing, which Custodia told the court was not enough, The Block reports.

For EU readers, this is a US banking question, but it shapes how easily US crypto firms, and the stablecoins they issue, can reach central-bank money. In the EU, MiCA requires stablecoin issuers to keep a large share of their reserves as deposits at commercial banks, so bank access matters there too.
