**Short version:** Huaisong Xiang, a 30-year-old former Robinhood software engineer, pleaded guilty on Monday, 16 November, to one count of wire fraud. US prosecutors say he used confidential knowledge of upcoming Robinhood token listings to trade perpetual futures on Hyperliquid, making $125,523. The case shows that trading on a decentralized platform does not put insider dealing out of reach of the law.

## What happened

Xiang entered his plea before US District Judge Colleen McMahon in New York, according to the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York. He agreed to forfeit $125,523. Sentencing is set for 25 February 2027. The charge carries a maximum of 20 years in prison, but that is a legal ceiling and the judge will decide the actual sentence.

Prosecutors say Xiang knew whether and when Robinhood Crypto would add new tokens. Between 2025 and 2026, he repeatedly bought [perpetual futures](/glossary) tied to those tokens on Hyperliquid before Robinhood announced the listings. Prosecutors say this breached his duty to keep the information confidential.

The FBI complaint filed in September gives more detail, as reported by Decrypt:

- Xiang worked on new listings and had access to a private Slack channel for staff classed as "Coin Aware Individuals".
- A November 2024 email warned him not to abuse that information.
- Before a planned 13 March 2025 listing, he allegedly moved about 18 ETH, then worth about $34,000, into a Hyperliquid wallet and opened a bullish position in POPCAT.
- The complaint identifies at least 10 more occasions through February 2026, including trades in MEW, MOODENG, ONDO and RENDER.

Robinhood's policy barred designated staff from trading on any platform before a listing announcement and for 24 hours after it, The Block reports. Robinhood told Bloomberg it investigated the matter, reported it to authorities and has zero tolerance for insider trading, according to The Block.

A second former Robinhood engineer was charged in September over separate trades. Those charges remain allegations, and the Justice Department's announcement gave no update on his case, Cointelegraph reports.

## Why it matters

**Decentralized does not mean anonymous.** Hyperliquid's trades are recorded onchain, so a wallet's history stays visible long after the trade. In May, the analytics firm Kaiko flagged suspicious Hyperliquid activity ahead of Robinhood's 15 January listing of Lighter (LIT), Cointelegraph reports. The public record gives investigators something to follow.

**The legal theory doesn't depend on the venue.** Prosecutors built this case around an employee misusing his employer's confidential information, so it did not matter where the trades took place. US Attorney Jamie McDonald said insiders will be held accountable "no matter the trading platform". Xiang pleaded to wire fraud, and the announcement did not report a plea to the commodities fraud charge first brought against him, Decrypt notes.

**It is a different outcome from this week's other big crypto fraud case.** In a ruling reported on Monday, a federal judge acquitted the Peraire-Bueno brothers in the [$25 million Ethereum bot case](/blog/peraire-bueno-acquitted-ethereum-mev-case-what-it-means-2026-11-17), ruling that prosecutors had not shown material misrepresentations. Xiang's case rested on a breach of duty to an employer, and he admitted the charge.

**Hyperliquid is the venue, not the accused.** The announcement alleges no wrongdoing by Hyperliquid, and neither The Block nor Decrypt carried a comment from it. Even so, the case adds to the attention on how large onchain perpetuals markets are used. For background, see our [Hyperliquid hub](/hyperliquid).

## What this means for you

- **A price jump before a listing can mean someone knew first.** If you trade small tokens around exchange listings, the other side of your trade may hold information you don't have.
- **Be careful copying "smart" wallets.** A wallet with a remarkable record of timing listings may be lucky, skilled or trading on inside knowledge. You can't tell from the outside.
- **EU rules ban this too.** MiCA, the EU's crypto rulebook, includes its own prohibition on insider dealing in crypto-assets. See [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained).
- **If you work in crypto, your employer's listing plans are not yours to trade.** That applies on any platform and through any wallet.

## What to watch

- The sentence on 25 February 2027, which will show how heavily a US court weighs a case of this size.
- What happens in the case against the second former engineer.
- Whether exchanges tighten staff trading rules, and whether regulators bring similar cases over onchain perpetuals.
