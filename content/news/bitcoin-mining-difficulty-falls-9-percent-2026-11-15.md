**Bitcoin's mining difficulty fell 9.13% to 134.82 trillion at block 975,744, early on Saturday, 14 November, according to TokenPost and Bitcoin Magazine. It is the largest cut since June.**

Difficulty measures how much computing work miners need to do to find a new block. The network resets it every 2,016 blocks, roughly every two weeks, so that blocks keep arriving about every 10 minutes. When miners switch machines off, blocks come more slowly and the next adjustment makes mining easier.

This cut was bigger than the 7.85% expected a few days earlier, TokenPost reports. It follows two increases in October, of 5.92% and 5.54%, which had taken difficulty to 148.36 trillion. The last larger drop was on 14 June, when difficulty fell 10.09%.

Neither outlet gave a cause, but the cut comes as bitcoin's price has fallen about 14% in a week, which squeezes what miners earn per unit of computing power. Some listed miners have also been leasing power to AI data centres this autumn, as [Riot did](/news/riot-28-billion-ai-lease-bitcoin-miners-split-2026-11-05). Easier difficulty gives the miners who stay online a little relief.

For EU readers: difficulty changes don't affect what you pay to send or hold bitcoin, but they are a useful sign of how much pressure miners are under.
