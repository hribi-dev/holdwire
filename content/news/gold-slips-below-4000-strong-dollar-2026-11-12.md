**Spot gold fell 0.4% to $3,973.13 an ounce early on Thursday, back below the $4,000 mark it had held through Tuesday's sell-off, according to Investing.com. US gold futures were down 0.6% at $3,978.57 and silver lost 0.6% to $51.36.**

The pressure comes from the dollar and interest rates. The US dollar index reached 102.87, its highest level since 9 April 2025, after rising 0.6% on Wednesday, Reuters figures published by Business Standard show. Fed funds futures now price an 87.3% chance of a quarter-point rate hike at the Federal Reserve's 9 December meeting and a 12.7% chance of a half-point hike. Gold pays no interest, so higher rates make it less attractive, and a stronger dollar makes it more expensive for buyers paying in other currencies.

Fed officials have added to the hawkish tone. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called October's inflation report "borderline disturbing", Investing.com reports, after US consumer prices rose more than expected on Tuesday.

For buyers in the euro area, a stronger dollar means the drop looks smaller when gold is priced in euros. The next test is the US producer price index on Friday, 13 November.
