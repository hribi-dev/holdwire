**ECB board member Piero Cipollone told the European Parliament's economic affairs committee on Tuesday that lawmakers should finish negotiating the digital euro legislation by the end of 2026, warning that a delay "could instead postpone" its introduction, crypto.news reports.**

If the law is agreed this year, the ECB says a first digital euro could be issued in 2029. A 12-month pilot with a test version, without legal tender status, would start in the second half of 2027. It would involve ECB and national central bank staff, selected merchants and 36 payment providers chosen in July from more than 50 applicants, according to crypto.news.

The bill is now in talks between the Parliament and EU governments. The Council agreed its position in December 2025, and the Parliament adopted its own on 9 July 2026 by 416 votes to 169. Parliament's position caps how much digital euro one person can hold, so that money does not drain out of commercial banks, Coincub reports; no figure for the cap was given. Negotiators last met on 5 November without a final deal.

Cipollone argued Europe relies too much on foreign card schemes, saying two-thirds of online card payments in Italy use non-European networks. For EU users, the digital euro would be a public, central-bank form of digital cash, separate from crypto and from private euro stablecoins like those regulated under MiCA.
