**Visa plans to let eligible payment companies in Asia-Pacific fund cross-border payouts on Visa Direct with stablecoins, starting with a pilot with Singapore payments firm dtcpay, according to Visa's announcement on 18 November.**

Visa Direct is the network businesses use to push money to cards and accounts, for example to pay gig workers or send remittances. Payment providers normally have to deposit money in advance, through banks, which close at weekends and on holidays. With stablecoin prefunding they can top up at any hour. The people receiving the money still get ordinary local currency.

Triple-A, a licensed digital-asset firm, converts the stablecoins and handles compliance screening and settlement, The Block reports. Its chief executive, Eric Barbier, said Asia-Pacific accounts for nearly a third of global cross-border payment flows. Visa's release does not name which stablecoins will be accepted; its earlier tests used Circle's USDC and EURC, The Block notes.

The move follows a US rollout with Inyo Global in October. Availability varies by country, Visa says.

For EU readers, this is a back-office change, not a new consumer product. It shows stablecoins being used where they are most practical today: moving money between businesses outside banking hours.
