**Short version:** Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Santander, Deutsche Bank and other firms have committed about $270 million to a joint company that will issue a bank-backed dollar stablecoin, Bloomberg reported on Tuesday, according to CoinDesk and PYMNTS. The venture, known internally as Project Faro, aims to launch in the first half of 2027 and plans a euro-pegged token next. It is the clearest sign yet that big banks want a share of a market now dominated by Tether and Circle.

## What happened

According to Bloomberg, as reported by CoinDesk, the banks have pledged about $270 million to cover five years of the new company's running costs. PYMNTS reports that the company is still looking for a chief executive and has not settled on a final name. None of the banks commented.

The plan itself is not new. In early September, 21 financial firms announced they would form a company to issue a dollar stablecoin, Decrypt reported at the time. The list includes ten North American firms (among them Goldman Sachs, Bank of America, Citi, Wells Fargo and Fidelity), eight from Europe (Santander, BBVA, Commerzbank, Crédit Agricole, Deutsche Bank, Lloyds, Rabobank and UBS), and MUFG, Sirius International Holding and Standard Bank. JPMorgan is not part of it. What Tuesday's report adds is money: the funding shows the project has moved from an announcement to a company with a budget.

The token would be backed by reserves the issuer holds, not by a central bank, Decrypt reports. The company is to be incorporated in the US. The group says it intends to comply with the US GENIUS Act and, "as applicable", the EU's MiCA rules. Cross-border payments and settling trades in digital assets are the first planned uses.

## Why it matters

Stablecoins are still a two-player market. Unchained, using DefiLlama data, puts dollar stablecoins at about $293 billion, with Tether's USDT at about $176 billion and Circle's USDC at about $70 billion: roughly 84% between them.

Banks see that money as theirs to lose. Every euro or dollar parked in a stablecoin is money that is not sitting in a bank deposit. A token issued by a group of regulated banks could appeal to companies and institutions that want stablecoin speed but are wary of a single crypto-native issuer.

It also won't be alone. Unchained notes that JPMorgan, Citi, Bank of America and Wells Fargo are separately working on a tokenized deposit network for 2027, and the Open USD stablecoin backed by Coinbase, Visa and Stripe has about $924 million in circulation. In Europe, PYMNTS reports that Qivalis, a consortium of European banks building a euro stablecoin, received an e-money licence from the Dutch central bank last month.

## What this means for you

- **Nothing changes yet.** There is no token to buy or use. The launch target is the first half of 2027, and the euro version comes after that.
- **A euro token from big banks would need MiCA authorisation.** Under MiCA, a stablecoin offered to EU users must be issued by an authorised e-money institution or bank. That is why some dollar stablecoins are limited on EU platforms. See [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained).
- **Bank-issued does not mean deposit-insured.** A stablecoin is a claim on the issuer's reserves, not a bank account. Check how reserves are held and reported before relying on any stablecoin for savings.
- **For payments, competition is good news.** More regulated euro stablecoins could mean cheaper cross-border transfers. Our [fintech section](/fintech) compares the cards and apps you can use today.

## What to watch

- **The company's name, CEO and blockchain.** None has been announced, and Unchained notes it is unclear which network the token will use.
- **Formation.** The banks said they would form the company in the second half of 2026, subject to closing conditions.
- **The euro race.** Whether Project Faro's euro token or Qivalis reaches EU users first.
