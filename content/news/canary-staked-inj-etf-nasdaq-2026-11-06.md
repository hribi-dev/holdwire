**Canary Capital's Staked INJ ETF, ticker INJC, began trading on Nasdaq on 5 November, the first US-listed fund built around staked Injective (INJ), according to The Defiant.**

The fund plans to stake at least 90% of its INJ "under normal circumstances", with BitGo as custodian, Altcoin Buzz reports, citing the fund's SEC filing. It charges a 1.10% annual sponsor fee, and The Defiant says it expects to keep 70% of staking rewards before that fee. Staked INJ has a 21-day unbonding period, so the fund keeps some tokens unstaked to handle redemptions.

The start was modest. About 2,933 shares changed hands on day one, according to TechFlow, citing Yahoo Finance data, worth a few hundred thousand dollars. TechFlow compared that with about $56 million on Bitwise's Solana staking ETF's first day. The fund's assets were about $15 million, largely from a seed investment by the Injective Foundation. INJ rose about 9% within hours of the launch, TechFlow reports, before Thursday's broad sell-off.

US crypto ETFs are not sold to most retail investors in the EU, where similar products are listed as ETPs. What the launch shows is that staking is becoming a standard feature of US crypto funds, and that fees and reward-sharing can take a large slice of the headline yield.
