**The grace period is over.** Since 1 July 2026, any company offering crypto services to people in the EU must be authorised under MiCA. Firms that were still operating under older national rules had to wind down or get licensed.

## Where things stand

ESMA's interim register grew quickly after the deadline: it listed more than 300 authorised crypto-asset service providers by late July, including banks such as BNY's Belgian unit alongside crypto-native firms. Some large names received authorisation only days after the deadline. The register is updated as national regulators report new licences, so the list keeps changing.

## Your four-step check

1. **Find the legal entity** behind your app, in its terms or footer. The brand name isn't enough.
2. **Look it up in ESMA's MiCA register.** No entry means no MiCA authorisation.
3. **Check the services listed.** A firm authorised only for custody isn't automatically authorised to run a trading platform.
4. **Check ESMA's non-compliant entities list**, which names firms regulators have flagged.

## If your provider isn't authorised

ESMA has told unauthorised firms to have orderly wind-down plans and has warned customers to move assets to an authorised provider or a wallet they control. Don't wait for a firm to sort it out on its own.
