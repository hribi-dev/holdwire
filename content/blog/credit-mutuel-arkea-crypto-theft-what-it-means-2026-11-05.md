**Short version:** Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a large French cooperative bank, has confirmed a cyberattack that let criminals make fraudulent transfers from the bank's own account and cash the money out through crypto exchanges. On-chain investigator ZachXBT puts the losses at more than $133 million (about €115 million), and the bank has accepted that order of magnitude. The bank says customers' money was not affected. Paris prosecutors have opened an investigation.

## What happened

The first signs appeared on-chain, not in a bank statement. On Monday, 2 November, ZachXBT reported a theft of more than $106 million without naming the victim. Data firm Bitquery found that $108.8 million in USDC, a dollar stablecoin, was withdrawn from accounts at Kraken, Crypto.com and OKX over about 21 hours on 1 and 2 November, according to CoinDesk.

ZachXBT later found another $27 million in withdrawals, taking the total above $133 million. He said the figure could rise further once over-the-counter trades are counted. On Wednesday he named Crédit Mutuel Arkéa as the suspected victim, and the bank confirmed the attack the same day.

According to the bank's statement, reported by The Block and Quartz, the attack enabled fraudulent transfers from the bank's own account. The money went out to other banks and was then converted into crypto through specialised platforms. The bank called it a sophisticated attack from outside and ruled out human error. It said some of the funds have been blocked. It has not given a final figure.

How the attackers got in has not been disclosed. Investigators have not yet determined how the attacker gained access to the exchange accounts, CoinDesk reports.

## How the money moved

ZachXBT described a fairly standard laundering path, according to The Block. The attacker swapped USDC for ether, which unlike USDC cannot be frozen by a central issuer. Funds then went through instant-swap services and were partly converted into Monero, a privacy coin, and bridged across several blockchains.

The exchanges responded quickly. Crypto.com said it blocked funds linked to the incident. OKX said it spotted unusual activity in an institutional customer account, froze it and contacted the client. Kraken restricted the associated accounts and said its own systems were not compromised, according to The Cryptonomist.

ZachXBT said he had a "high degree of confidence" that North Korea's Lazarus Group was not behind it, citing how the funds were moved. That sets it apart from the $6.3 billion [Binance hack](/blog/binance-hack-what-it-means-2026-11-02), which the FBI attributed to North Korea.

## Why it matters

Most crypto thefts hit crypto companies. This one hit a traditional European bank, with crypto used as the exit. Crédit Mutuel Arkéa has more than 5.8 million members and customers and €211.5 billion in total assets, according to The Block. It said the amounts involved are in no way large enough to threaten its financial strength.

Speed is the second lesson. ZachXBT criticised the bank's response, saying that without a quicker report the attackers were able to launder more than $30 million that European authorities might otherwise have frozen, Quartz reports. Stablecoins like USDC can be frozen by their issuer, but only while they are still stablecoins. Once swapped into ether or Monero, that option is gone.

There is also a regulatory angle. In May, French financial outlet L'Agefi reported that the bank had applied for a [MiCA](/learn/mica-explained) licence to offer crypto services, CoinDesk notes. The attack does not appear to involve any crypto service the bank offers to customers.

## What this means for you

- **If you bank with Crédit Mutuel Arkéa** (or its brands), the bank says customer assets were not affected and no customer data was stolen. Some transfers were delayed. It has warned customers to watch for phishing, which often follows a public incident like this.
- **Expect scam messages.** Fraudsters use headlines. A call, SMS or email saying your account was "affected by the attack" and asking you to move money or confirm details is a red flag. Contact your bank through its official app or a number you already know. Our [guide to spotting scams](/school/spotting-scams) covers the common tricks.
- **Exchanges can and do freeze funds.** All three exchanges named took steps against the accounts involved. That is part of why regulated, [MiCA-authorised](/school/exchanges-and-mica) platforms matter: they have compliance teams and obligations to act on stolen funds.

## What to watch

- Whether the bank publishes a final loss figure and explains how the attackers got access.
- The Paris prosecutor's investigation, handled by France's anti-cybercrime office, according to Quartz.
- How much of the stolen money was blocked in total, by the exchanges or other banks along the way.
