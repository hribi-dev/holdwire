**European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde said on Friday that the EU needs common rules for tokenized financial assets soon, before blockchain platforms that cannot settle with each other split Europe's market.**

Speaking at the European Banking Congress in Frankfurt, Lagarde said tokenization could help build a real single market in digital assets, CoinDesk reports. But she warned the window is limited. "Common rules will be needed too, and those lie with legislators," she said, according to CoinDesk and Cointelegraph.

The market is still small but growing fast. Tokenized traditional assets on public blockchains were worth about 23 billion euros in March, five times as much as a year earlier, both outlets report.

The ECB is already building the plumbing. Its Pontes service, launched in September, settles tokenized securities in central bank money, and the European Stability Mechanism settled a 1 billion euro digitally native bond through it this month. The Eurosystem's Appia initiative is working on common standards, with a blueprint planned for 2028, Cointelegraph reports.

For EU readers, this is about bonds, funds and shares on blockchains, not about crypto coins, which MiCA already covers. If the rules land, buying tokenized EU securities across borders could become simpler. Lagarde is due to leave the ECB in February 2027.
