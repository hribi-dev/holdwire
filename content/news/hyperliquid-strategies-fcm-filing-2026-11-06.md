**Hyperliquid Strategies (Nasdaq: PURR), a company that holds HYPE tokens as its main asset, has applied to the CFTC to register a subsidiary as a futures commission merchant (FCM), according to The Block.**

An FCM is the US equivalent of a futures broker: it holds client accounts and money. If approved, the unit could carry accounts for US customers trading permissioned markets on Hyperliquid built under HIP-3, the framework that lets outside teams launch their own markets on the exchange, Blockonomi reports. The company said there is "no assurance" about the timing or outcome.

It is the latest step in a busy fortnight for Hyperliquid in the US. On 29 October, ICE, owner of the New York Stock Exchange, agreed to work with trade.xyz on Hyperliquid-style perpetual futures for US traders ([our brief](/news/ice-trade-xyz-hyperliquid-us)).

The company also reported results for the quarter to 30 September. Revenue was $13.3 million, above analysts' estimates, and net income was $620.6 million, mostly from $803 million in unrealised gains on its HYPE, according to The Block. It held about 38.5 million HYPE at the end of September. HYPE itself was down about 7% on Thursday, in line with the wider sell-off.

For EU readers, this changes nothing directly: it is a US licence. It does show how Hyperliquid is trying to reach regulated customers through partners rather than by registering itself.
