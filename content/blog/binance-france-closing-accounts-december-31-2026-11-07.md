**Short version:** Binance France has told customers it will close their accounts on 31 December 2026 because it did not obtain a licence under the EU's MiCA rules, according to reports by Cryptoast, The Cryptonomist and PANews. From 29 November, accounts that still hold crypto will be charged a monthly fee of 0.025% of the balance, with a minimum of 10 USDC. If you still have coins there, withdrawing them before 29 November avoids the fee entirely.

## What happened

Customers of Binance France SAS received emails in early November setting out the wind-down, The Cryptonomist reports. The key dates, as reported by PANews and SpendNode:

- **29 November 2026:** the fee is calculated on any balance still in the account. The first deduction happens within three days.
- **After 24 December 2026:** a second deduction.
- **31 December 2026:** accounts are closed.

The fee is 0.025% of the balance per month or 10 USDC, whichever is higher. It is taken from USDC first and then from other coins in the account. Deposits are already switched off. PANews reports that assets still in an account after 31 December may be treated as inactive and could be converted or transferred.

The reason is MiCA. Since the transition period ended on 1 July 2026, crypto firms need authorisation in an EU country to serve customers across the bloc. Binance did not have one by the deadline. From 1 July it stopped spot and margin trading for French users and left withdrawals open, crypto.news reported at the time, adding that Binance had about 2 million users in France. According to The Cryptonomist, France's AMF removed Binance France from its list of registered crypto providers (PSAN) on 2 July.

## Why it matters

This turns a long pause into a hard deadline. For four months, French customers could leave coins on Binance and wait for a licence. Now, money left there costs something and the account will be closed.

The fee hits small balances hardest because of the 10 USDC minimum. SpendNode works through the maths: on a 100 USDC balance the first charge would be 10 USDC, or 10% of the balance; on 1,000 USDC it is 1%; only from 40,000 USDC does the 0.025% rate itself apply.

The reports so far concern Binance France only, not Binance's global business. Binance also restricted services elsewhere in the European Economic Area from 1 July, and Tech Insider reports it was still not listed as an authorised MiCA provider as of 3 November. Co-CEO Richard Teng has said Binance expects authorisation "in the coming months", without giving a date.

## What this means for you

If you live in France and have a Binance account:

1. **Withdraw before 29 November.** Reports say a full withdrawal before then avoids all fees. SpendNode reports a deduction is capped at what is left in the account, so small leftovers could simply be swept away: empty the account completely.
2. **Choose where the coins go.** Either a MiCA-licensed exchange (check it in ESMA's register, see our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-explained) and [exchanges and MiCA](/school/exchanges-and-mica)), or a wallet you control ([wallets and seed phrases](/school/wallets-and-seed-phrases)).
3. **Send a small test first.** Check the network and address, and add a memo or tag if the receiving exchange asks for one. A wrong network is the most common way to lose coins in a move like this.
4. **Keep your records.** Download your transaction history before the account closes. You need it for French tax returns on crypto gains.
5. **Ignore "migration" offers.** Expect fake emails and messages about moving your Binance balance. Log in only through the app or a bookmark you trust. See [spotting scams](/school/spotting-scams).

If you live elsewhere in the EU, nothing in these reports changes your account today. Still, check which company you contract with and whether it is licensed.

## What to watch

- Whether Binance confirms the dates and fees publicly, not just by email.
- Whether Binance gets MiCA authorisation in another EU country, and whether that would let French customers back in.
- What happens to assets still in accounts after 31 December.
