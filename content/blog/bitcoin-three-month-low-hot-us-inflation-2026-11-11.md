**Short version:** US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, more than the 0.3% economists expected, and core inflation also beat forecasts. Markets now treat a Federal Reserve rate hike in December as a done deal, and some traders are betting on a bigger one. Bitcoin slipped below $73,000 on Tuesday, its weakest level since 20 August, according to CoinDesk, while ether, XRP and Solana fell close to 6%.

## What happened

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics published October's consumer price index at 14:30 CET on Tuesday. Headline prices rose 0.4% on the month, down from 0.6% in September but above the 0.3% forecast. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 0.3% against a forecast of 0.2%, the third month in a row at that pace, Decrypt reports. Shelter costs made up more than 30% of the monthly rise. There is no year-on-year figure this time, because no data was collected in October 2025 during the US government shutdown.

The reaction in rates was fast. According to CoinDesk, CME FedWatch showed an 85% chance of a quarter-point hike at the Fed's 8–9 December meeting and a 15% chance of a half-point hike shortly after the release. Earlier in the week, the odds of no change had been above 30%; they went to zero. Fed Governor Christopher Waller had already said he would back a December hike even if inflation cooled, and that another 0.3% core reading could justify 50 basis points. The two-year US Treasury yield had its biggest one-day rise since April 2025, Decrypt says, and CoinDesk reports the ten-year yield climbed above 5%.

Bitcoin was near $74,200 before the data, according to Decrypt, and dipped to about $73,525 within minutes. About $178 million in leveraged long positions were liquidated in an hour. By late morning in New York it had broken below $73,000, a new low for this cycle, CoinDesk reports. The Block had ether at $2,042, down 3.7%, and Solana below $83. KuCoin's daily report put bitcoin at $73,804 and ether at $2,053 at midnight UTC.

## Why it matters

Crypto has spent most of 2026 trading as a bet on cheap money. Higher rates work the other way: they make cash and bonds pay more, and they raise the cost of borrowing to buy risky assets. A Fed that is raising rates rather than cutting is a headwind, and Tuesday's numbers made that more likely.

The selling also comes on top of weeks of outflows. US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $201 million on Monday alone, according to Farside data cited by CoinDesk, and about $1.5 billion since 2 November. Ether ETFs lost $185 million the same day, KuCoin reports, the third day of outflows in a row for both. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index fell to 29 from 32.

Notably, bitcoin did not follow stocks up on Monday, when the S&P 500 had its best day since April. That break with equities is something CoinDesk flagged before the data came out.

## What this means for you

- **Macro data now moves crypto.** US inflation and Fed meetings matter as much as crypto-specific news right now. Know the dates (below) if you hold coins.
- **Leverage is the fastest way to lose.** $178 million in long bets were wiped out in an hour on a 0.1-point surprise. If you don't know your liquidation price, don't trade with borrowed money. Our [glossary](/glossary) explains the terms.
- **Euro holders see a slightly different picture.** Higher US rates tend to strengthen the dollar, which can soften the fall in euro terms or make it worse when the dollar weakens again. Check the price in the currency you actually use.
- **Stick to authorised platforms.** In the EU, check your exchange is licensed under MiCA before moving money in a volatile week. See [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained).
- **Analysts' "support levels" are not promises.** Bitget's Lewis Huang told The Block the 200-day moving average near $73,200 is a level traders watch. That says where buyers have stepped in before, not what happens next.

## What to watch

- **US producer prices on Thursday, 13 November**, and remarks from Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson on 14 November.
- **The Fed decision on 9 December.** The next CPI report comes on 10 December, the day after.
- **MSCI's index review on Wednesday evening.** CryptoTicker reports MSCI will name companies dropping out under its new rules for firms with large crypto holdings, with Strategy among the candidates.
- **ETF flows.** A turn back to inflows would be the first sign that large investors are returning.
