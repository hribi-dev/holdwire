**The European Commission has launched an EU Crypto Sanctions Task Force to detect and stop attempts to evade EU financial sanctions using crypto assets, Financial Services Commissioner Maria Luís Albuquerque announced.**

The announcement came at the tenth high-level meeting on sanctions implementation on 5 November, where Albuquerque also announced a new EU sanctions office to speed up guidance to member states and improve data sharing, according to the Kyiv Independent. The task force will bring together national and EU experts to design ways to close loopholes, and will draw on experience from Ukraine and the UK. An EU diplomat told the Kyiv Independent it is planned to run for 12 months at first.

"We will not allow new technologies or third-country jurisdictions to become safe havens for sanctions evasion," Albuquerque said in the Commission's statement. Commission spokesperson Siobhan McGarry said crypto has been used to get around EU sanctions.

The step follows earlier sanctions packages aimed at Russia's crypto sector, including the A7 network behind the A7A5 rouble stablecoin, Bitcoin.com News reports. The same week, the US cut off the MEXC exchange over alleged sanctions-linked flows ([our explainer](/blog/us-treasury-cuts-off-mexc-what-it-means-2026-11-07)).

For ordinary EU users, little changes directly: MiCA-licensed exchanges already screen transfers. But expect stricter checks on transfers linked to high-risk platforms and countries.
