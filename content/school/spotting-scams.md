## Why scams work

Scammers don't need you to be careless. They use pressure, excitement and trust. Crypto is a favourite because payments are fast and usually can't be reversed.

## The most common scams

- **Fake support.** You post a question online, and "support" messages you privately. They ask for your login, a 2FA code or your seed phrase, or ask you to "verify" your wallet on a website. That site steals everything.
- **Guaranteed returns.** "Our trading bot makes 3% a day." No real investment guarantees high returns. Early "profits" are often paid from newer victims' money (a Ponzi scheme) until it collapses.
- **Romance and friendship scams.** Someone you met online slowly builds trust, then introduces you to an "investment platform". The platform shows growing profits but never lets you withdraw.
- **Impersonation.** Fake accounts of famous people or companies promising giveaways: "Send 1 ETH, get 2 back." Nobody gives away free money this way.
- **Recovery scams.** After you've lost money, someone offers to get it back for a fee. It's the same scammers, or new ones.

## Rules that protect you

1. **Never share your seed phrase, password or 2FA codes.** With anyone. Ever.
2. **Type addresses yourself.** Don't click links in messages or ads to reach exchanges or wallets. Use bookmarks.
3. **Slow down.** If someone pushes you to act now, the answer is no.
4. **Check the licence.** In the EU, crypto firms must be authorised under MiCA. If you can't find a firm on the official registers, don't send it money.
5. **Talk to someone.** Scammers ask for secrecy. Telling a friend breaks the spell.

## If it happens to you

Stop sending money. Report it to your bank (if you paid by card or transfer), the exchange you used, your local police and your national financial regulator. Keep screenshots and transaction IDs. And ignore anyone who contacts you offering to recover the funds.
