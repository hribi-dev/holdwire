**Gold rose nearly 2% on Monday as oil prices fell on hopes of a US–Iran deal, then slipped about 0.5% to roughly $4,120 an ounce on Tuesday morning as traders waited for US inflation figures, according to Trading Economics.**

Brent crude dropped 5.8% on Monday, KuCoin's daily report says, after progress in US–Iran talks brokered with proposals from Qatar and Pakistan. Cheaper oil eases inflation worries, which supports gold because it lowers the pressure on the Federal Reserve to keep raising rates. A softer dollar helped too, Yahoo Finance reports. Silver traded around $53.29 on Tuesday, according to Trading Economics.

The next test comes on Tuesday at 14:30 CET, when the US publishes October consumer prices. FinanceFeeds reports that futures markets were pricing about an 81% chance of a Fed rate hike in December after Friday's strong jobs report; Trading Economics puts it at about 84%. Higher rates make holding gold, which pays no interest, relatively less attractive. Gold remains about 27% below its January record of $5,608, FinanceFeeds notes.

For euro-based buyers, gold's moves in dollars are only part of the picture: a weaker dollar can soften gains when converted into euros.
