**Short version:** Binance lost about $6.3 billion in the largest crypto theft on record. It says it will cover all customer losses, has frozen about $3 billion of the stolen funds, and reopened withdrawals on Sunday. Customers pulled out a net $3.64 billion on the first day, which is a lot of money but a small share of what Binance holds. Bitcoin is steady near $78,000.

## What happened

On the evening of 29 October, an attacker moved roughly $6.36 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in about 20 minutes, according to blockchain analytics firm TRM Labs. Binance's own estimate is about $6.3 billion.

Binance says its early findings point to a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer. That gave the attacker access to an internal service that could submit withdrawals. The exchange says it found no evidence that wallet private keys were leaked.

## Who is behind it

TRM Labs says, "with high confidence", that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group. Chainalysis's CEO and on-chain investigator ZachXBT point the same way. Binance itself has not named anyone and says it is working with law enforcement. The same group was blamed by the FBI for the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in February 2025.

## What Binance has done

- **Paused withdrawals**, then reopened them on Sunday 1 November in phases, starting with bitcoin.
- **Froze about $3 billion** of the stolen funds with help from blockchain partners.
- **Promised to cover all losses** from its SAFU emergency fund and company money. It says it has already replaced about 95% of the stolen funds from its own reserves.
- **Promised an updated proof of reserves** and a technical report.

## The run that didn't happen

In the first 9.5 hours after withdrawals reopened, Binance processed $4.649 billion of withdrawals and $1.01 billion of deposits: a net outflow of about $3.64 billion. By Monday morning, net withdrawals had reached about $4.7 billion, which Bloomberg reported as under 4% of the exchange's assets.

The market reaction was also smaller than many expected. Bitcoin fell about 4.5% to near $76,700 when the news broke, and more than $1.5 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated. By Monday it was back near $78,000.

## What this means for you

You don't need to panic, but this is a good moment for three checks:

1. **Don't keep more on any exchange than you need to trade.** An exchange holds your coins for you. If it is hacked or fails, you depend on its promise to pay you back. Coins you're holding for the long term can sit in a wallet you control.
2. **Check that your exchange is authorised to serve you in the EU.** Since 1 July 2026, crypto firms serving EU residents need a MiCA licence. Authorised firms have to keep customer assets separate and meet capital and security rules. [Our MiCA guide](/learn/mica-explained) shows how to check in two minutes.
3. **Watch out for scams.** Big hacks are followed by fake "refund" emails, fake support accounts and fake recovery services. Binance will not ask for your password, seed phrase or 2FA codes.

- **Binance's proof of reserves and technical report**, which will show whether its numbers add up.
- **US midterm elections on 3 November.** Control of Congress could shape pending crypto laws.
- **US jobs report on 6 November**, a big driver of risk appetite.
- **A Hyperliquid token unlock on 6 November** worth about $16.8 million, around 0.08% of supply.
