**The total value of all stablecoins has fallen to about $263 billion, the lowest level in more than a year, according to on-chain analyst Darkfost, as reported by TokenPost on 3 November.**

## What happened

Stablecoin market value peaked at roughly $280 billion in April, so about $17 billion, or around 6%, has left in six months, by Darkfost's measure. He links the fall to weak demand and little new capital coming into crypto, and says repeated hacks may be making investors less willing to take crypto risk. The reports do not name the data provider behind the figures.

Binance stands out. It holds about 70% of the stablecoins sitting on centralised exchanges, but its 90-day average stablecoin balance has fallen from about $51 billion to $34 billion. Darkfost describes that as a trend lasting more than a year, TokenPost reports, while TechFlow adds that about $3 billion in stablecoins left Binance after its record hack.

## Why it matters

Stablecoins are the cash that traders use to buy crypto. When their total supply grows, new money is usually arriving. When it shrinks, money is leaving or sitting on the sidelines. Darkfost warns that a continued lack of inflows could reverse the positive trends of recent months.

## What it means for you

This is a measure of market liquidity, not a sign that stablecoins themselves are failing. If you hold stablecoins in the EU, check that the token is MiCA-compliant and the platform holding it is authorised.
