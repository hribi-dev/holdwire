**Short version:** Mercado Bitcoin, Brazil's largest crypto exchange, says it detected and contained a "security incident" early on Monday 2 November and paused deposits and withdrawals. On-chain researchers estimate that between $40 million and $48.5 million left its hot wallets. The exchange says no customer lost money and no data leaked. It has not said when withdrawals will reopen.

## What happened

According to the exchange's own statement, reported by Seu Dinheiro, Mercado Bitcoin found a security incident in one of its systems on Monday morning and contained it quickly. As a precaution it suspended deposits and withdrawals, and temporarily limited access to its app, according to PYMNTS, which relayed a Bloomberg report.

The exchange has not published an official loss figure. Outside researchers have:

- **Hypernative**, a blockchain security firm, first flagged more than $40 million in suspicious transfers, according to Cyber Security News.
- **Bitquery**, a blockchain data firm, counts $48.5 million taken across 10 blockchains, valued at the time of each transfer. It says Ethereum accounted for $29.5 million and Bitcoin for about 106 BTC.
- Bloomberg, via PYMNTS, reported that the company will absorb a **$40 million** loss.

Researchers quoted by Cyber Security News say most of the outflows happened in a window of roughly half an hour on Monday morning (UTC), and that they looked like ordinary signed transfers, not a smart-contract bug. In other words, the attacker appears to have been able to sign transactions from the exchange's own wallets. How they got that access is not known.

Bitquery also reports that the attacker emptied a large number of small customer deposit addresses later in the day, worth about $261,000 on Ethereum, after the exchange had said the attack was contained. Mercado Bitcoin has not commented on that.

## Where the money is

Bitquery says stablecoin issuers Tether and Paxos froze about $7.3 million of the stolen funds. At the time of its check, most of the rest had not moved to any exchange. Mercado Bitcoin has published the destination addresses so other platforms can block them.

Nobody has been named as the attacker. There is no public evidence linking this breach to last week's $6.3 billion Binance hack, and researchers have been careful not to claim one.

## Why it matters

Two exchange breaches in five days, both through hot wallets, both apparently through signing access rather than a software bug. The amounts are very different: Mercado Bitcoin's loss is less than 1% of Binance's. But the pattern is the same one investigators have pointed to all year. Attackers go after the people and systems that approve withdrawals, not the blockchain itself.

The good news, for now, is that both exchanges say customers will not bear the loss. The less good news is that this promise depends entirely on the exchange having the money, which is why the next few days matter.

## What this means for you

Most EU readers won't have a Mercado Bitcoin account. The lessons still apply to any exchange:

1. **A paused withdrawal is the main risk.** Even when no customer loses money, you can't move your coins until the exchange reopens. Keep only what you trade on an exchange. Coins you hold for the long term can sit in a wallet you control. Our [glossary](/glossary) explains hot wallets, cold wallets and self-custody.
2. **Check who regulates your exchange.** In the EU, crypto firms need a MiCA licence. Licensed firms must keep customer assets separate and meet security rules. [Our MiCA guide](/learn/mica-explained) shows how to check.
3. **Ignore "recovery" offers.** After every hack, scammers pose as support staff or recovery services. A real exchange will never ask for your password, seed phrase or 2FA codes.

## What to watch

- **A reopening date** for deposits and withdrawals, and whether Mercado Bitcoin confirms an official loss figure.
- **A post-incident report** explaining how the attacker got signing access.
- **More freezes** by stablecoin issuers. Bitquery noted that some USDC and EURC was still free to move at its last check.
- **Binance's next steps**: more withdrawals were due to reopen on 3 November and a new reserve snapshot is planned for 4 November. We explain [what its proof of reserves does and doesn't show](/blog/binance-proof-of-reserves-explained-2026-11-03).
