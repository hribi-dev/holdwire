**Payward, the parent company of Kraken, plans to run regulated perpetual futures markets for US clients on Hyperliquid's infrastructure.** It would be the first CFTC-regulated firm to deploy markets there. The plan still needs regulatory approval.

## How the structure works

The design uses HIP-3, Hyperliquid's system that lets outside firms launch their own markets:

1. **Bitnomial deploys the markets.** Bitnomial is Payward's CFTC-regulated exchange and clearinghouse, acquired earlier this year. It would create, own and run the markets, and clear and settle the contracts.
2. **NinjaTrader Clearing holds client accounts.** It is Payward's CFTC-registered futures broker.
3. **Access is permissioned.** Only traders onboarded by NinjaTrader and placed on both firms' allowlists could trade. Hyperliquid's existing open order books stay separate.

## Why it matters

Talks between Payward and Hyperliquid were first reported in August. The new plan is more concrete, and it arrives days after ICE announced its own US plans with trade.xyz. Together they show two different regulated routes into the US for Hyperliquid's technology, both run by licensed firms rather than by Hyperliquid itself.

## What to watch

- **CFTC approval** for either plan.
- **Which markets** are offered first, and at what leverage.
- **Whether similar structures appear in Europe**, where a provider would need MiCA authorisation.
