**Spot gold fell below $3,600 an ounce on Friday morning, 20 November, to around $3,579, according to Mitrade, and CNBC reports it is set for its worst week in eight months.**

The slide continues a sharp run of losses. Gold dropped 2% to $3,667 on Thursday, its weakest since September 2025, CoinDesk reports, a day after a fall of more than 3%. Silver lost 4.71% to about $42.83, Mitrade says.

The main driver is the US Federal Reserve. Markets now price about a 90% chance of a quarter-point rate rise at the Fed's 8–9 December meeting, according to Mitrade, and the dollar is near a 19-month high. Gold pays no interest, so higher rates and a stronger dollar make it less attractive. Mitrade also points to heavy selling of tech stocks, with some institutions reportedly selling commodities to hold cash.

Other pressures it lists are tension around the Strait of Hormuz and softer US data, including 203,000 weekly jobless claims against 197,000 expected.

For EU readers, gold is priced in dollars, so the strong dollar has softened the fall in euro terms.
