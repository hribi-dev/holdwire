**President Donald Trump has nominated four commissioners to the two main US market regulators, including Republican Steven Levine to replace Hester Peirce, who left the SEC in October, Cointelegraph reports.**

The White House named two picks for the Securities and Exchange Commission. Levine's term would run to June 2030; Cointelegraph notes his views on crypto are less publicly known. Democrat Kristin Johnson, a former commissioner at the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, is nominated for the seat once held by Caroline Crenshaw, with a term to June 2029. At the CFTC, Johnson argued for stronger customer protections and conflict-of-interest rules for crypto firms.

For the CFTC, Republican Nathan Anonick and Democrat Matthew Rowland are nominated. Both worked on the CLARITY Act, the US crypto market-structure bill. CFTC chairman Michael Selig, currently the agency's only commissioner, called them "two strong bipartisan picks".

All four need Senate confirmation. The SEC has only two of its five seats filled, and Democrats take control of the Senate in January, leaving Republicans a short window to confirm the picks, according to Cointelegraph.

For EU readers, these agencies do not regulate EU firms directly. But they decide how US exchanges, ETFs and stablecoin rules develop, and those choices shape what reaches European markets.
