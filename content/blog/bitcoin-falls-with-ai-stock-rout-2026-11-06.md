**Short version:** Bitcoin fell from above $80,000 to below $76,000 on Thursday as a sell-off in US chip and AI stocks spread to crypto. Ether dropped about 7.5%, and more than $1 billion in leveraged crypto bets were liquidated in 24 hours, according to CoinGlass data. The midterm rally we reported [a day earlier](/news/bitcoin-back-above-80000-after-midterms-2026-11-05) is gone.

## What happened

Bitcoin had held near $80,000 through Wednesday, and a short squeeze early on Thursday pushed it past $80,500, according to Bitcoin Magazine. Within about two hours of the US stock market opening, it was more than $4,000 lower. CoinDesk put the 24-hour fall at roughly 5.5%, taking bitcoin back to where it was before Wednesday's rally.

Other large coins fell harder. According to CoinGecko data cited by crypto.news, ether fell 7.5% to about $2,251, Solana 7.9% to $89.16 and XRP 6.1% to $1.23. BNB held up better, down 3.9%. Total crypto market value fell 4.6% to about $2.66 trillion. The big dollar stablecoins kept their pegs.

The trigger came from the stock market. The Nasdaq fell 5.5% and the S&P 500 4.3%, the worst session since the April 2025 tariff shock, CoinDesk reports. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12%, its worst day since March 2020. Kitco reported that the S&P 500 closed down 4.36%.

## Why stocks fell, and why crypto followed

Two worries hit at once. After Democrats won both houses of Congress on Tuesday, investors started pricing in tighter rules on the AI build-out, including possible pauses on new data-centre construction. UBS analysts estimated that about 30% of the US data-centre capacity expected in 2028 could be at risk from such pauses, Bitcoin Magazine reports, citing Reuters. Weak forecasts from chipmakers added pressure, said Carlos Guzman of GSR, quoted by CoinDesk.

Crypto is tied into that story in two ways. First, many bitcoin miners now rent their power and buildings to AI companies, so they carry the same political risk. Mining stocks fell 15% to 20%, according to CoinDesk. Second, bitcoin has been trading more like a tech stock. A CryptoQuant measure of bitcoin's correlation with the S&P 500 turned positive early on Thursday for the first time in data going back to 2015, CoinMarketCap Academy reports.

Leverage made the move sharper. CoinGlass data cited by crypto.news show $1.08 billion in liquidations over 24 hours, of which $954 million were bets on rising prices. More than 210,000 traders were liquidated. The single largest was a $10.94 million ether position on Binance. CoinDesk called it the biggest wipeout since 28–29 October, the days after the [Binance hack](/blog/binance-hack-what-it-means-2026-11-02).

Crypto shares fell further. Coinbase, Strategy, Robinhood and Circle each lost more than 10%, according to crypto.news.

Gold went the other way. It touched about $4,148 an ounce, its highest since 20 October, before settling around $4,087, up 0.6%, Kitco reports. Falling Treasury yields helped.

## What this means for you

- **Leverage is what turns a bad day into a wipeout.** Most of the losses came from people trading with borrowed money. If you hold coins outright, a 5% drop is painful but it does not close your position for you. Our [glossary](/glossary) explains liquidation and funding.
- **Don't treat bitcoin as a hedge against stocks right now.** On days like Thursday it has moved with the Nasdaq, not against it. That may change, but the data this week point the other way.
- **Prices in euros.** Bitcoin and ether are priced in dollars, so the euro moves too. Check the [live prices](/coins) in your own currency before you react to a headline.
- **Make a plan before the next swing.** Decide in advance what you would do if prices fell another 10%. Decisions made in a panic are usually the expensive ones.

## What to watch

- **The US jobs report today (Friday).** A strong number could keep the Fed leaning towards another rate rise in December, which tends to weigh on risk assets. Kitco says the chance of a December hike priced by markets fell to about 66–69% after Thursday's sell-off, from about 83% on Wednesday.
- **US inflation data on 10 November** and the Fed decision on 9 December.
- **The $73,000–$76,000 zone.** Glassnode sees support there, according to CoinMarketCap Academy. Analysts disagree about what happens if it breaks, and we don't make predictions.
- **Whether chip stocks stabilise.** For now, that seems to be what crypto is following.
