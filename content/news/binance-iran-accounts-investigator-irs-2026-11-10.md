**A former senior financial crimes investigator at Binance has told the US tax authority, the IRS, that the exchange's leadership ignored calls to investigate about 2,000 accounts suspected of being used by people in Iran, which allegedly moved about $1.2 billion, Forbes reported on Monday.**

According to Forbes, the internal probe was code-named "Operation Loclock". The investigator and several colleagues were suspended after looking into two accounts that allegedly sent money to Iran in breach of US sanctions, and later left the company. His lawyer says there is substantial evidence that Binance helped funds move from China to Iran. The US Justice Department and the IRS are investigating Iranian use of the platform, focused on payments for Iranian oil, and have issued subpoenas to Binance and former staff, Forbes reports. No charges have been filed.

Binance says it broke no Iran sanctions, that it fired the investigators for breaching its customer data privacy rules, not for raising concerns, and that it cooperates fully with law enforcement, according to Forbes and Coin Edition. A spokesperson told the New York Times the account investigation was never shut down.

The allegations come as Binance is still dealing with the October hack. For EU users, nothing changes for now, but the story is a reminder to know which legal entity holds your account and which regulator supervises it.
