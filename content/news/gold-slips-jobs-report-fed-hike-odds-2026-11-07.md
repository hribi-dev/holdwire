**Gold fell about 0.9% to roughly $4,051 an ounce in early US trading on Friday after US employers added 165,000 jobs in October, nearly three times the roughly 65,000 economists expected, according to Kitco.**

The report was strong across the board. Unemployment fell to 4.1% from 4.2%, average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month, and August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000 jobs, Kitco reports. Interest-rate futures moved to about an 80% chance of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate increase at the 8–9 December meeting, up from roughly 75% before the data, according to Reuters figures cited by KuCoin News.

Higher rates tend to weigh on gold, which pays no interest. The Rio Times reports the US dollar index rose 0.72% to 102.28 and the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.87%, and puts gold's close at about $4,045, down 1.02%. Silver held up better, trading near $51.86, up slightly, in Kitco's early figures.

The move gives back only part of the week's gains: gold was still up about 4.4% over five sessions, after jumping back above $4,000 on Wednesday ([our brief](/news/gold-back-above-4000-silver-jumps-2026-11-05)). For EU buyers, a stronger dollar also changes the euro price, so the move in euros can differ from the dollar headline.
