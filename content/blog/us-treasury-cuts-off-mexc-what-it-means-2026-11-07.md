**Short version:** On Friday the US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) named MEXC Global, one of the world's 20 largest crypto exchanges, a "primary money laundering concern" and ordered US financial institutions to stop all transfers to and from it. FinCEN says MEXC moved about $16.7 billion linked to Russian sanctions evasion, Iran, North Korea and cybercrime between May 2021 and May 2026. The order is a US measure, but it is a strong warning sign for anyone holding money on the platform, wherever they live.

## What happened

FinCEN published the order on 6 November, signed by deputy director Jimmy L. Kirby. It uses Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act, not an ordinary sanctions listing, Bitcoin Magazine reports. Once it takes effect, covered US financial institutions may not process any transfer of funds, including crypto, to or from MEXC. The ban starts 30 days after the order appears in the Federal Register, which had not happened by Friday evening US time, and it has no end date.

The order covers MEXC Global Ltd (also MX Global Ltd) in the Seychelles, **MEXC Estonia OÜ** in Estonia, GIDA Technology AG in Switzerland and MXC Global Ltd in the UK, plus successor companies, according to the FinCEN document.

FinCEN's own figures, as set out in the order:

- about $1.5 billion in direct and indirect exposure to Russian sanctions evasion, including about $232 million in direct transactions with US, EU or UK sanctioned entities;
- about $129 million with Garantex and $4.1 million with its successor Grinex, two sanctioned Russian exchanges, with dealings as recently as June 2026;
- nearly $200 million tied to the rouble-linked A7A5 token;
- about $1.87 billion in Iran exposure, including over $1 billion directly with more than 40 Iranian exchangers and about $22 million linked to Iran's central bank and the IRGC;
- at least $1.99 billion from cyber criminals and more than $37.9 million in suspected ransomware payments.

FinCEN describes MEXC as handling over $1 billion a day, holding about $5 billion in reserves and having roughly 9% of global centralised exchange market share as of December 2025. MEXC had not responded publicly by the time The Block, Bitcoin Magazine and Techreport published. The Block notes that MEXC has said it runs anti-money-laundering controls and blocks users in countries such as Iran and North Korea; FinCEN says those controls had "severe deficiencies".

## Why it matters

Bitcoin Magazine points to Bitzlato, a Russian-linked exchange, as an earlier Section 9714 case; MEXC, by FinCEN's own numbers, is a far bigger platform. Treasury frames it as part of a wider campaign it calls Operation Economic Outcast, which Techreport says targets offshore exchanges by cutting their access to dollar banking rather than freezing assets. Earlier targets include Iran's Nobitex and the Russian A7 network.

Being cut off from US banks matters even for an exchange that does not serve Americans (MEXC's terms already list the US as a prohibited country). Market makers, payment partners and stablecoin issuers that touch the US system will have to decide whether they can keep working with it. MEXC has also had warnings before: the UK's FCA put it on its unauthorised-firms list in March 2024, Hong Kong's SFC warned about it the same month, and the Seychelles regulator acted against MX Global Ltd in May 2026, according to Bitcoin Magazine.

## What this means for you

If you are in the EU and use MEXC, the order does not by itself freeze your account or stop you withdrawing. But it raises the risk around the platform. A few practical points:

- **Check the register.** Since 1 July 2026, firms serving EU customers need a MiCA licence. Look the exchange up in ESMA's register before you leave money there. Our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-explained) shows how.
- **Watch for knock-on effects.** If banking or stablecoin partners pull back, deposits, card top-ups or euro withdrawals can become slower or stop. Problems usually show up there first.
- **Don't leave more on an exchange than you need.** Coins you are not trading are safer in a wallet you control. See [wallets and seed phrases](/school/wallets-and-seed-phrases).
- **Be wary of "help" messages.** Big news about an exchange brings fake support accounts offering to move or "unlock" funds. Use only the official app or site.

## What to watch

- When the order is published in the Federal Register, which starts the 30-day clock.
- Any public response from MEXC, and whether it changes its banking or stablecoin partners.
- Whether EU or UK authorities take their own action, given that one of the named companies is registered in Estonia.
- Whether Treasury names more exchanges under the same campaign.
