**The hackers behind the $6.3 billion Binance theft on 29 October likely had access to a Binance engineer's device for about eight weeks beforehand, the nonprofit Security Alliance (SEAL) estimates, according to Cointelegraph.**

SEAL links the attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korea-linked group, which the FBI also named. Its report traces a campaign in stages, as summarised by Cointelegraph: in June, an employee installed a tampered software package while setting up an AI coding tool, which Binance detected and cleaned up. Around September, a software engineer's device was compromised through a fake recruitment process. On 20 October, attackers used that machine and an internal AI tool to take over the devices of two wallet infrastructure engineers and found a deployment permission token, which was then used in the theft. Binance told Cointelegraph the attackers did not reach wallet operations until 20 October.

SEAL named front companies used in fake recruiting this year, including CitaLab, BitFit and Oddium-related domains. It found similar patterns elsewhere: about six weeks of access before the $388 million Bitget theft and about three weeks before the $24 million AFX exploit, according to Crypto Economy.

The lesson for ordinary users is the same as after every exchange hack: the weak point was people and devices, not the blockchain. Coins you aren't trading are not exposed to an exchange's internal security if they sit in your own wallet. Unsolicited "job offers" with a coding test are a common way in for attackers.
