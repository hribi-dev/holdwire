**Short version:** Gold fell again this week and has now dropped in 10 of the last 12 weeks, matching its worst run of weekly losses in almost four decades, according to BullionVault. The London benchmark price ended Friday around $3,890 an ounce, its lowest end-of-week level since October 2025. The main reasons are the same ones hitting bitcoin: US bond yields jumping, a stronger dollar and markets betting the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates in December.

## What happened

- **The price.** Spot gold opened the week at about $4,066, touched $4,149 on Monday, then slid to a low of about $3,852 on Thursday morning, according to Kitco. By Friday afternoon it was near $3,884, down 5% from the Sunday open. Measured from one Friday benchmark to the next, the drop was 3.5%, BullionVault reports.
- **The streak.** Ten weekly falls in twelve weeks equals gold's worst stretch in almost 40 years, BullionVault's Adrian Ash writes. The closest comparison is spring 1989, when gold fell in 11 weeks out of 12.
- **Thursday's drop.** Gold lost 3% on Thursday to its lowest level in more than a year, after rising oil prices and hotter-than-expected US inflation data, Reuters reported. We covered the earlier moves in our briefs on [the inflation shock](/news/gold-falls-nearly-3-percent-hot-us-inflation-2026-11-11) and [the strong dollar](/news/gold-slips-below-4000-strong-dollar-2026-11-12).
- **Silver did worse.** Silver dipped below $50 an ounce in late London trade and fell 3.8% on the week. It is now more than 50% below its record Friday close in late January, according to BullionVault. Platinum was near $1,512, Reuters reported.

## Why gold is falling

Gold pays no interest. When safe investments that do pay interest become more rewarding, holding gold costs more in missed income, and some investors switch.

That is what happened this week. The 10-year US Treasury yield rose 0.27 percentage points to 5.13%, its steepest weekly rise since April 2025, BullionVault reports. Yields adjusted for inflation rose almost as much, to 2.87%. CoinDesk reported the dollar index above 103, its highest since April 2025, which makes dollar-priced gold more expensive for buyers using other currencies.

Behind the yields is inflation. October's consumer price data came in hot, and producer prices excluding food and energy were up 5.0% on a year earlier, the fastest pace since early 2023, according to BullionVault. After Friday's producer price figures, markets put the chance of a 25 basis point Fed hike in December at 89.4%, down slightly from 96.2% before the release, Reuters reported. Gold "may struggle to retake 4,000 with Fed hawks out in force," Tai Wong told Reuters.

Gold and bitcoin are falling together. Both are often described as hedges against loose money, and right now money is getting tighter. Bitcoin is down more than 21% this year, according to CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk.

## What professionals and readers think

In Kitco's weekly survey of 13 Wall Street analysts, 62% expected gold to fall further next week, 15% expected a rise and 23% saw it moving sideways. Kitco's online poll of 160 readers was split, with 42% expecting a rise and 38% a fall. These are opinions, not forecasts we endorse.

## What this means for you

- **Check the price in euros.** Gold is quoted in dollars. When the dollar strengthens against the euro, as it has recently, the fall in euro terms is usually smaller than the headline dollar figure.
- **Long losing streaks happen.** The 1989 comparison is a reminder that gold can fall for months, even after a big run. Anyone holding it as long-term insurance should be comfortable with that.
- **Physical gold, ETFs and tokens all follow the same price.** A gold bar, a gold ETF or a gold token such as [Tether Gold](/news/tether-gold-xaut-kpmg-audit-2026-11-13) does not protect you from the price falling, only from the risks of the wrapper you chose.
- **Mind the spread.** In volatile weeks, the gap between buying and selling prices for coins and small bars can widen. Compare before you trade.
