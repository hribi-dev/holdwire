**Spot gold traded near $3,853.10 an ounce late on Tuesday, 17 November, down 0.06% on the day. On Monday it touched its weakest level since October 2025.**

Silver did better. It rose about 1% to near $47.33 an ounce as bargain hunters stepped in after Monday's sharp losses, Kitco reports. Silver had fallen on Monday to its lowest since late October 2025, according to Reuters.

The main pressure on gold is the outlook for US interest rates. Markets priced about a 94% chance of a 0.25-point Federal Reserve rate rise at the 8–9 December meeting, and about 6% for a 0.5-point rise, Kitco reports. Higher rates make gold, which pays no interest, less attractive by comparison. The dollar also reached a 19-month high on Tuesday, according to Reuters, which makes gold more expensive for buyers using other currencies. It later eased from those highs, Kitco reports.

The next test comes today, Wednesday, when the Fed publishes the minutes of its 27–28 October meeting.

For EU readers, gold is priced in dollars, so a strong dollar can soften the fall in euro terms. The return on a gold holding depends on both the metal's price and the euro-dollar exchange rate.
