**Coinbase-owned derivatives exchange Deribit stopped all trading for 19 minutes on Friday afternoon, hours after a planned pause to install a security patch, as crypto prices slid.**

The first pause came at 09:00 UTC, when Deribit deployed what it called a patch for a "recently disclosed vulnerability", crypto.news reports. Trading was meant to stop for under five minutes and resumed at 09:06 UTC.

The second was unplanned. At 16:17 UTC Deribit's status page said trading was unavailable and the exchange was investigating. Trading resumed at 16:36 UTC, according to crypto.news. The trading lock covered all currencies, including bitcoin, ether, Solana and XRP perpetuals, stock-linked perps and gold and silver perps, The Defiant reported. Customers could cancel orders, but not close positions, since that needs a trade. Deribit did not give a cause.

The timing was awkward. Bitcoin was heading below $60,000 that day, and Coinbase had opened Deribit-powered options to non-US customers on Coinbase Advanced only a day earlier.

For EU traders the lesson is simple: during an outage you cannot cut a leveraged position, but margin rules can still apply. If you trade derivatives, keep extra margin and know your exchange's rules for outages.
