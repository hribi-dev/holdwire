**Analytics firm Inca Digital traced $518 million of trades from flagged wallets through Sky's stablecoin swap module, about $101.9 million of it directly linked to North Korean hackers, CoinDesk reports.**

Sky, formerly MakerDAO, runs a Peg Stability Module (PSM). It lets anyone swap Circle's USDC for Sky's DAI or USDS at a fixed 1:1 rate. Circle can freeze USDC held by a blacklisted address; DAI and USDS have no such freeze. Inca called the module an "exit from freezable USDC". It has no identity checks, and 95% of the flagged trades came in through swap aggregators such as 1inch, ParaSwap and MetaMask Swaps, according to the report.

The flagged flows came from 422 wallets linked to hacks, phishing and sanctioned entities. After the February 2025 Bybit hack, $91.5 million went through the PSM in ten days, almost $30 million of it after the FBI had publicly blamed North Korea, CoinDesk reports.

The report also points to a wider risk. About $2.7 billion of USDC held by Sky's module sits in a single Ethereum wallet set up through Coinbase Prime, CoinDesk reports. Circle can only freeze a whole address, so a blacklisting would lock all of it.

For EU users of DAI or USDS, the point is that a stablecoin is only as safe as its reserves, and part of Sky's reserves could be frozen by another company's decision.
