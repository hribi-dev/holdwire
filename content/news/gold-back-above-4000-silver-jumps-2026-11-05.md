**Gold rose $136.50, or 3.47%, to about $4,066 an ounce on Wednesday, and silver climbed 5.25% to about $52.28, according to Kitco.**

## What happened

A day earlier gold had slipped to about $3,924 on strong US data ([our brief](/news/gold-slips-below-3930-yields-rise-2026-11-04)). Wednesday reversed that. After Democrats won both houses of Congress, investors expected gridlock and less government spending, Kitco reports, and the dollar, Treasury yields and oil all fell. The dollar index dropped about 0.3% to near 101.5, according to the Investing News Network, and the 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.90%. Lower yields make gold, which pays no interest, cheaper to hold.

Hopes of renewed US-Iran talks also cut the risk premium in oil. Brent crude fell about 8.7% to $94.62, Kitco says.

## Context

Gold is still well below its January peak: its highest daily close this year was about $5,513 on 28 January, according to The Rio Times. Both metals hit 12-month lows on 30 October. Commentators disagree on what a divided Congress means. Peter Schiff argued it could be bullish for gold if it leads to bigger deficits, while Rich Checkan said the election matters little unless it brings major spending cuts, the Investing News Network reports.

## What it means for EU readers

Much of the move was a weaker dollar, so the gain in euros is smaller. Friday's US jobs report is the next test ahead of the Fed's 8–9 December meeting.
