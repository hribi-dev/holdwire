**A US federal appeals court ruled 2-1 on Tuesday, 17 November, that Kalshi cannot stop Maryland from applying its gambling law to the prediction market's sports contracts.**

The Fourth Circuit upheld a district court's decision not to grant Kalshi a preliminary injunction, DeFi Rate and InGame report. The two judges in the majority gave different reasons. Judge Roger Gregory found that the federal Commodity Exchange Act does not override state gambling laws. Judge Stephanie Thacker found that sports event contracts are not swaps at all. Either finding is enough for Maryland to win. Judge DeAndra Gist Benjamin dissented, according to InGame.

The Fourth Circuit is the third appeals court to side with a state, while the Third Circuit ruled for Kalshi in New Jersey. That split makes a Supreme Court case more likely. New Jersey has already asked the Supreme Court to hear its case, and Kalshi's reply is due on 7 December, InGame reports. Kalshi can also ask the full Fourth Circuit to rehear the case. A spokesperson said the judges "can't agree with each other on why we're wrong".

For EU readers, the ruling changes nothing directly. It shows that even in the US it is still unsettled who regulates prediction markets: the federal CFTC or individual states.
