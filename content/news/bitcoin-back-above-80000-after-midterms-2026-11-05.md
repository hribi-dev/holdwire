**Bitcoin rose to about $80,450 on Wednesday, up roughly 3.65% in 24 hours, and briefly touched $81,000, according to CoinDesk, reversing its election-night drop to just below $76,000.**

## What happened

The fall came overnight as Democrats won both houses of Congress (see [yesterday's analysis](/blog/democrats-win-congress-what-it-means-for-crypto-2026-11-04)). By the US open, bitcoin had already recovered to about $78,300, and it crossed $80,500 mid-morning New York time. Ether gained about 6% and traded near $2,426, while XRP and SOL rose about 3.5% each, CoinDesk reports.

Analysts quoted by CoinDesk said the result was largely expected and that the end of the uncertainty brought buyers back. Short sellers were caught out: CoinGlass data showed more than $378 million in short positions liquidated within one hour, according to Interactive Crypto.

## Context

US stocks rallied too, with the Dow up about 3%, its best day since April 2025. Not all crypto-linked shares joined in. Several bitcoin miners that have pivoted to AI data centres fell more than 10%, after Democratic wins in Texas and Ohio. ETF demand also remains weak: US spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.16 billion over the three trading days to 2 November.

## What it means for EU readers

Bitcoin is now trading above where it stood before the results came in, but a weaker dollar means the gain looks slightly smaller in euros. Friday's US jobs report on 6 November is the next big data point for markets.
