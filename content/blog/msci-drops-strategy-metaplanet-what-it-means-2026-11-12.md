**Short version:** Index provider MSCI said on Wednesday evening that it will remove Strategy, the company that holds more bitcoin than any other, from its USA index at the close on 30 November. That also takes it out of the MSCI World and MSCI ACWI, the benchmarks behind many of the ETFs Europeans buy in savings plans. Metaplanet, Japan's largest bitcoin treasury company, is being dropped too. Funds that track these indexes will sell their shares; nobody is forced to sell bitcoin.

## What happened

MSCI published the results of its November index review after the US market closed on 11 November. According to Bitcoin Magazine, Strategy (ticker MSTR) will be deleted from the MSCI USA Index and therefore from the MSCI World and ACWI. The same review removes Metaplanet from the MSCI Japan Small Cap Index, Upexi from the MSCI USA Micro Cap, uranium holder Yellow Cake from the UK Small Cap and Iceland's Skel from a frontier-markets index. Strategy alone made up more than 90% of the combined float-adjusted value of the five, Bitcoin Magazine says.

The removals follow a new rule for "non-operating companies" that MSCI adopted on 15 October. Under it, a company needs operating assets worth at least 50% of its total assets. If it fails that test, it is checked against five financial ratios, and failing four or more makes it ineligible. Current members are only removed if they fail in both of their last two annual reporting periods. According to investingLive, Strategy had already used up that buffer, so it went straight to the deletion list. MSCI's documents gave no company-specific reasons, The Block reports.

The rule replaces an earlier plan, floated in late 2025, that would have targeted any company holding 50% or more of its assets in crypto. MSCI dropped that in January and consulted again in August. Seventeen other companies were on the watch list, including SharpLink, TON Strategy and Solana Company, but none of them were deleted this time, according to Bitcoin Magazine.

Strategy shares closed at $122.35 on Wednesday, up 1.06%, then fell after the announcement. Reported after-hours losses ranged from about 1.5% (Bitcoin Magazine) to 2.6% (The Block). Metaplanet had already closed 2.53% lower at 231 yen before the news, The Block says.

## How big is the selling?

Smaller than the headlines from last year suggested. Strategy says funds tracking MSCI's global indexes hold about 13 million of its shares, around 3.1% of its basic shares and less than one day's normal trading volume, Cointelegraph reports. A widely quoted JPMorgan estimate of about $2.8 billion in outflows, or up to $8.8 billion if other index providers followed, dates from November 2025 and was made for MSCI's earlier, narrower proposal. MSCI has not published its own figure.

Index funds usually trade close to the closing price on the effective date, so any pressure is likely to bunch up around the close on 30 November, CryptoTicker notes.

Strategy has fought the rule throughout. Executive chairman Michael Saylor and chief executive Phong Le called the treatment of digital asset treasury companies discriminatory, The Block reports, and the company told MSCI in an earlier letter that a deletion would have no meaningful impact on its business.

## Why it matters

Strategy held 850,364 bitcoin as of 8 November, bought at an average of $75,456 each, according to Bitcoin Magazine. With bitcoin trading around $73,800 overnight, those holdings were about 2.2% below their average cost, CryptoTicker calculates. The stock is down about 20% this year and bitcoin about 15%, investingLive says.

Index membership matters for companies like this because passive funds are steady, price-insensitive buyers. Losing them makes it a little harder to raise money by selling new shares, which is how Strategy has funded most of its bitcoin purchases. It also marks the first time a major index provider has formally treated a bitcoin treasury company as something other than an ordinary operating business.

## What this means for you

- **If you hold an MSCI World or ACWI ETF:** you don't need to do anything. Your fund will sell its Strategy shares automatically at the rebalance. Strategy is a tiny part of these indexes, so the effect on your ETF is negligible.
- **If you hold MSTR or Metaplanet shares:** they keep trading as normal. What changes is that one group of automatic buyers disappears. Expect possibly heavier trading around 30 November.
- **If you hold bitcoin:** index funds sell shares, not coins. The link to bitcoin is indirect, through sentiment and through how easily treasury companies can keep buying.
- **Remember what a treasury stock is.** A company that holds bitcoin adds its own risks on top of bitcoin's: debt, new share issues and management decisions. It is not the same as owning the coin. Our [glossary](/glossary) explains terms like mNAV and dilution.

## What to watch

- **The close on 30 November**, when the deletions take effect.
- **Other index providers.** FTSE Russell and S&P have their own rules, and MSCI's decision does not bind them, CryptoTicker notes.
- **The 17 companies on MSCI's watch list**, which could face the same test at future reviews.
- **Strategy's weekly purchase reports**, a guide to whether it keeps buying while its holdings sit below cost. Track the bitcoin price on our [coins page](/coins).
