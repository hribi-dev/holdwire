**Short version:** Democrats won control of both the US House and the Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections, according to NBC News projections. Bitcoin fell to about $75,700 overnight, its lowest level in roughly two months, then recovered to near $77,000. The bigger effect is on US crypto law: the CLARITY Act, which the Senate already rejected in September, now looks unlikely to pass before the new Congress starts on 3 January 2027.

## What happened

Bitcoin traded near $77,966 late on Tuesday evening, European time. As results came in, it slid to a low of $75,740 at 04:10 German time on Wednesday, according to CryptoTicker, a fall of about 2.9%. That low came about five minutes before CBS News called the House for the Democrats. NBC News later projected that Democrats had won the Senate as well.

On Polymarket, the odds of Democrats winning both chambers rose from about 75% at midnight to 99% before the House call, CryptoTicker reports. Stock futures fell too: Nasdaq-100 futures dropped about 2% over the evening, according to CNBC figures cited in the same report. By early Wednesday morning Bitcoin was back near $76,900 (about €68,400).

The election was not the only weight on prices. US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $399 million on 2 November, the second heavy day of withdrawals in a row, according to KuCoin's daily report. Traders were also paying more for short-term protection than long-term protection ahead of the results, a sign they expected a sharp move.

## Why it matters for crypto

**The CLARITY Act is probably out of time.** This is the bill meant to draw a clear line between crypto tokens that count as securities and those that count as commodities. The House passed it in July 2025, 294 to 134. The Senate rejected it 49 to 50 on 15 September 2026. Unless the Senate acts again, the bill expires when the current Congress ends on 3 January 2027, CryptoTicker notes. A new version would have to start from scratch in a Congress where Democrats, who blocked the bill in September, hold both chambers.

**Rules shift to the regulators.** Stablecoins stay covered by the GENIUS Act, which is already law. The SEC and CFTC can keep writing rules and guidance. Several analysts quoted by Cryptonews say that can still give the industry some clarity, but agency rules are easier for a future government to reverse than an act of Congress.

**More scrutiny of the Trump family's crypto ventures.** Rep. Robert Garcia, who is set to chair the House Oversight Committee, has said he will investigate what he calls Trump family corruption, including its crypto businesses, according to Cryptonews. CryptoTicker reports that Rep. Maxine Waters, a critic of those ties, is in line to chair the House Financial Services Committee.

Not everyone sees this as bad news. One Decrypt writer, quoted by Cryptonews, said the first-order take for crypto is "not that bad", because the regulators remain friendly and stablecoin law is settled.

## What this means for you

If you live in the EU, your rules have not changed. Crypto firms serving you still need a MiCA licence, and stablecoins sold to you still need to meet MiCA's rules. Our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-explained) covers what that means.

US politics still matters to your portfolio, because US investors and ETFs drive much of the Bitcoin market. Expect US crypto news over the next two months to focus on two things: whether anything can still pass before January, and what the SEC and CFTC do on their own. Overnight swings like this one are a reminder that leverage is risky around big scheduled events: a 3% move is enough to wipe out a position at 35x.

## What to watch

- **Final Senate count.** Some races were still being counted on Wednesday morning, and certification can take days.
- **Lame-duck Congress.** The current Congress sits until 3 January. Watch for any last attempt to revive market-structure legislation.
- **US jobs report on 6 November.** Strong economic data has kept a Federal Reserve rate rise in play for December, which matters for Bitcoin as much as politics does.
- **ETF flows.** Two days of heavy outflows came before the result. Whether they continue now that it is known is the next signal.
