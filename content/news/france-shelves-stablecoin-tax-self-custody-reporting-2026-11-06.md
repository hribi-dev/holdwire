**France's National Assembly rejected the revenue section of the 2027 budget by 458 votes to none late on Wednesday, which drops several crypto tax measures from the bill for now, according to Cointelegraph.**

Two crypto amendments are affected. One, adopted on 16 October by 38 votes to 37, would have taxed some crypto-to-stablecoin conversions from January 2027, covering stablecoins classed as e-money tokens under MiCA, crypto.news reports. Today, most crypto-to-crypto swaps in France are not taxed until you cash out. The other, approved on 3 November, would have required anyone holding at least €100,000 in self-custodied crypto on 31 December to declare it. It was a reporting duty, not a new tax. A third amendment would have raised fines for platforms that fail to answer tax authorities from €10,000 to €50,000, according to Cointelegraph.

This is a pause, not the end. The government sends the Senate its original text without the crypto amendments. Senators begin the budget debate on 20 November and reach the revenue articles on 23 November, when the measures could be reintroduced. The budget must be passed by 17 December, crypto.news notes, and the government can still force it through with Article 49.3.

For French crypto users, nothing changes yet. If you keep large amounts in your own wallet, it is worth following the Senate debate. The same week, Greece passed a 10% crypto tax ([our explainer](/blog/greece-crypto-tax-10-percent-what-it-means-2026-11-06)).
