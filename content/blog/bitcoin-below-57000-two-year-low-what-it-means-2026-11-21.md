**Short version:** Bitcoin fell below $57,000 late on Friday, its lowest price since September 2024, as a sell-off in tech stocks spread to gold, copper and crypto. About $2.4 billion of leveraged crypto positions were closed out in 24 hours, according to CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk. Nothing about how bitcoin works has changed; this is a risk-off market, and leverage made it sharper.

## What happened

Bitcoin broke below $57,000 on Friday evening US time, CoinDesk reported, the first time in more than two years. Reuters put it at $55,830 at 04:13 GMT on Saturday, down 5.10% on its previous close. Bitcoin had already dropped below $60,000 earlier on Friday for the first time since July.

The fall is part of a longer slide. Bitcoin is down 28% this quarter, according to CoinDesk, and the bear market began after it peaked above $126,000 in October 2025.

Other coins fell harder. Ether was around $1,543 on Saturday morning, down 10.69% in 24 hours, according to CoinGlass data cited by Brave New Coin. XRP slipped to about $0.87. On CoinDesk's own price pages on Saturday, Solana was down close to 10% and Hyperliquid's HYPE more than 13% over 24 hours.

Leverage made the drop sharper. CoinGlass counted $2.40 billion of liquidations across centralised and decentralised exchanges in 24 hours, CoinDesk reported. More than $1 billion came from bitcoin futures and $545 million from ether futures. The largest single liquidation was a $51.71 million bitcoin long. CoinDesk said most traders had been positioned for a bull market.

## Why it happened

Crypto did not fall alone. The Nasdaq dropped more than 4% on Friday to its lowest since April, and gold hit its lowest since September 2025, CoinDesk reported. Analysts pointed to rising inflation-adjusted US Treasury yields and a more hawkish outlook for the Federal Reserve. Reuters said worries about "lofty valuation" in the AI trade were weighing on risk assets broadly.

Money is also leaving the bitcoin funds that carried the last rally. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) had a net outflow of $541.1 million on 19 November, its largest single day since launch in January 2024, according to Farside data cited by TFTC. All US spot bitcoin ETFs together lost $582.3 million that day. Bloomberg also reported the record outflow.

Put together: when investors cut risk across the board, crypto tends to fall with tech stocks, and forced selling from leveraged traders adds to the move. Liquidations amplify a fall; they rarely start it.

## What this means for you

- **If you hold coins without leverage, a liquidation cascade does not touch you directly.** Your coins are not sold for you. What you see is a lower price, not a forced exit.
- **If you trade with leverage, this is the risk in action.** Exchanges close positions automatically once your margin runs out, often at the worst price. Our [glossary](/glossary) explains liquidation, margin and funding.
- **Don't let a sharp move rush you.** Big drops bring scam messages promising "recovery" or guaranteed rebounds. See [spotting scams](/school/spotting-scams).
- **Check where your coins are.** In a fast market, exchanges can lag or pause. If you use an EU exchange, make sure it is authorised under MiCA; our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-explained) shows how.
- **Price and tax are separate.** In most EU countries a loss only counts for tax when you sell. Rules differ by country, so check your own.

- Whether outflows from US spot bitcoin ETFs continue next week, or ease.
- US tech stocks: crypto has been moving closely with the Nasdaq during this sell-off.
- Fed speakers and US yields ahead of the December rate decision.
