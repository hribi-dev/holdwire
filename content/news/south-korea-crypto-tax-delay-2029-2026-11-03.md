**Lawmakers from South Korea's ruling Democratic Party agreed on 3 November to recommend postponing the country's crypto income tax again, with a two-year delay to 2029 as their preferred option, crypto.news reports.**

## What happened

The decision came after a closed-door meeting between the National Assembly's finance committee and government officials. Under current law, the tax starts on 1 January 2027: individuals would pay 20% on yearly crypto gains above 2.5 million won, or 22% including local income tax. The recommendation is not final. Party leadership is expected to review it within one to two weeks, and changing the date needs an amendment to the Income Tax Act before 1 January.

## Why the delay

Lawmakers say they still support taxing crypto but need more preparation time, according to crypto.news. They point to gaps in data from offshore exchanges: South Korea starts swapping information under the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) in 2027. A pending Digital Asset Basic Act could also change the rules, and exchange Bithumb said it needs six to nine months to build and test reporting systems. The opposition People Power Party has proposed a longer delay or scrapping the tax.

This would be the fourth postponement. The tax was first due in 2022.

## What it means for EU readers

Nothing changes for you directly. But CARF is the same framework behind the EU's DAC8 rules, under which EU crypto providers collect user data for tax authorities from 2026. Korea's debate shows how much tax enforcement now depends on that cross-border data.
