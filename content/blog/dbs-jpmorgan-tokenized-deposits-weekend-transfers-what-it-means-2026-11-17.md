**Short version:** Singapore's DBS and JPMorgan have connected their blockchain-based deposit systems, so approved corporate clients can move US dollars between the two banks in Singapore and the US at any hour, including weekends. The money stays a normal bank deposit, recorded as a token on a private, permissioned ledger. It is not crypto and not a stablecoin, but it shows where banks are heading as they compete with stablecoins for cross-border payments.

## What happened

On Monday, 16 November, DBS and Kinexys, JPMorgan's blockchain unit, said their networks can now talk to each other. DBS Token Services and Kinexys each let clients hold dollar deposits as tokens on the bank's own ledger. The new link lets those tokens move between the two banks instantly, around the clock, CoinDesk and The Block report.

Partior, a settlement network the two banks helped set up, coordinates the settlement, according to CoinDesk. The first client is Ant International, which will use the link to manage cash across its Singapore and US companies, The Block reports.

There is one gap. Transfers inside the Kinexys network run 24/7, but moving money between JPMorgan's ordinary accounts and its blockchain accounts pauses for three hours each Saturday, from 3 p.m. to 6 p.m. US Eastern time. The banks say a fix is in the works, CoinDesk reports.

The launch builds on a plan the banks announced in November 2025. It also feeds into Project BLOOM, a Monetary Authority of Singapore programme on making tokenized money safe and widely usable.

## Why it matters

Normal cross-border bank payments stop when payment systems close, usually overnight and at weekends. CoinDesk gives the example of a company with $5 million at DBS in Singapore that needs to pay a US supplier on a Saturday: until now, that money would sit until Monday.

Stablecoins such as USDT and USDC already move 24/7, which is one reason companies have started using them. Tokenized deposits are the banks' answer: the same always-on movement, but the money stays a regulated bank deposit on the bank's balance sheet, held only by approved clients. The banks quote figures showing why they care. Outbound cross-border payments from Asia are projected to reach $24 trillion by 2033, up from $13.5 trillion in 2025, according to Money20/20 and FXC Intelligence, as cited by CoinDesk. A DBS survey found about 88% of companies are using or piloting some form of tokenized money.

Other banks are moving the same way. JPMorgan brought its dollar deposit token, JPMD, to Ethereum for institutional clients in October, The Block reports. Swift said in July that 17 banks will pilot live transactions on its blockchain-based ledger, and US banks are working on a shared tokenized deposit network through The Clearing House, according to CoinDesk. On the same day, Standard Chartered said tokenized real-world assets could reach $2 trillion, helped by Japan's plans to tokenize government bonds, The Block reports.

## What this means for you

- **You can't use this directly.** It is for large corporate clients of the two banks. Your own bank transfers won't change because of it.
- **It is not a crypto product.** There is no token to buy and nothing to trade. Be wary of anyone selling a "JPMorgan coin" or "DBS token" to the public.
- **EU rules treat it differently from stablecoins.** MiCA, the EU's crypto rulebook, covers stablecoins but not bank deposits, so tokenized deposits stay under ordinary banking rules, including deposit protection where it applies. See [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained).
- **The race affects your fees over time.** Banks building always-on rails, and a 21-bank group planning its own stablecoin with a euro version (see [our explainer](/blog/banks-270-million-stablecoin-project-faro-what-it-means-2026-11-11)), are both trying to win payments that fintechs and stablecoins now take. ## What to watch

- Whether the Saturday gap on JPMorgan's side is closed.
- Whether European banks join similar links, for example through the Swift ledger pilot.
- Whether euro tokenized deposits appear alongside the dollar ones.
