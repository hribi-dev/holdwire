**South Korea's Financial Services Commission (FSC) has asked the country's crypto exchanges to move more customer assets into offline cold wallets, after an emergency meeting on 2 November prompted by the $6.3 billion Binance hack.**

## What happened

Officials met staff from Upbit, Bithumb, Coinone, Gopax and other operators, along with the Financial Supervisory Service and the exchange group DAXA, Unchained reports. Besides more cold storage, the FSC asked for round-the-clock monitoring of deposits and withdrawals, more incident-response staff, faster reporting of suspicious access and extra reserves.

The current rule requires exchanges to keep at least 80% of customer crypto offline. The FSC did not set a new number at the meeting, but Korean outlet News1 reported industry talk of raising it to about 99%. Upbit already keeps 99.17% offline, while Gopax's operator was at 80.54% at the end of September, according to regulator figures cited by Unchained.

Exchanges also asked the FSC to loosen limits on pausing withdrawals when another exchange is hacked, according to Yonhap, and the regulator plans to review those rules.

## Why it matters

Hot wallets are the online wallets exchanges use to pay out withdrawals quickly, and they are where most big thefts happen. The more an exchange keeps offline, the less an attacker can take in one go.

## What it means for EU readers

MiCA requires licensed exchanges to keep client crypto safe and separate, but it does not set a fixed offline percentage. Ask your platform how much it keeps in cold storage, and consider holding long-term coins in your own wallet.
