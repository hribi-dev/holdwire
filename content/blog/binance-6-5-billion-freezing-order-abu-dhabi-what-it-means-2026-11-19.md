**Short version:** A court in Abu Dhabi has granted Binance a worldwide freezing order of up to $6.5 billion against the unknown people behind its 29 October hack. Binance's lawyers have listed about 26,000 wallet addresses holding stolen funds. The order makes it easier for exchanges and stablecoin issuers to hold and hand back what they catch, but more than half of the stolen money has been turned into bitcoin and ether, which no court can freeze directly.

## What happened

Justice William Stone SBS KC of the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) Courts issued the order on Wednesday, 18 November, The Block reports. It covers unidentified defendants and comes with two other kinds of order: injunctions over specific stolen assets, and "delivery-up" orders that require those assets to be handed over.

Binance says the orders can be served via the blockchain, and the list of 26,000 addresses will be updated as the funds move. M. Kendall Day of the law firm Gibson Dunn, which acts for Binance, called it possibly the largest publicly known freezing order ever made, according to The Block.

The $6.5 billion is a ceiling, not money recovered. It is about 102.5% of the roughly $6.34 billion Binance says the attack affected, according to Blockchain Reporter. By 18 November, $2.85 billion, or 45%, had been frozen or intercepted, including $1.2 billion restrained by the US Department of Justice on 12 November. About $3.49 billion, or 55%, was still out of reach. Binance has not published the order itself.

TRM Labs and US government sources attribute the hack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean group, TFTC reports. Binance has already made customers whole, using more than $6.34 billion from its own treasury, its SAFU emergency fund and insurance.

## Why it matters

Freezing stolen crypto has two halves. Exchanges and stablecoin issuers such as Tether and Circle can lock funds that reach them. But many will only release those funds with a court order behind them, The Block reports. Elliptic's Tom Robinson said earlier that issuers would want "a clear legal basis" before reissuing frozen stablecoins to Binance, and that this could take weeks or months. A court order naming the wallets gives them that basis.

The limit is the other half. The hackers swapped stablecoins for bitcoin and ether, TFTC reports. Coins sitting in a self-custody wallet on those networks cannot be frozen by anyone. They only become catchable when they touch an exchange, a bridge or an issuer. As Robinson put it, each time the funds move "there is a chance to catch them".

BNB, the token tied to Binance, barely reacted. It closed at $596.85 on 18 November, 23.2% below its close on the day of the hack, but its 24-hour fall was in line with bitcoin's, according to Blockchain Reporter.

## What this means for you

- **Binance customers:** the exchange says balances were already restored. This order is about Binance getting its own money back.
- **Exchanges you use may freeze deposits** that trace back to listed wallets. If you ever receive crypto from a stranger or an OTC deal, its history can follow it. Our lesson on [spotting scams](/school/spotting-scams) covers how stolen funds get passed on.
- **Self-custody cuts both ways.** The same property that keeps your coins safe from seizure keeps stolen coins safe from recovery. Know where your coins are and who can freeze them. See [wallets and seed phrases](/school/wallets-and-seed-phrases).

## What to watch

- How much of the $3.49 billion still outstanding gets frozen as the funds move to exchanges.
- Whether stablecoin issuers start reissuing frozen tokens to Binance under the order.
- Courts in other countries recognising the ADGM order.
- More US seizures, after the earlier action covered in [our 14 November story](/blog/us-seizes-binance-hack-funds-facestealer-what-it-means-2026-11-14).
