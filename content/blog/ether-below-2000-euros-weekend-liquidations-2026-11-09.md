**Short version:** Late on Sunday evening, European time, ether fell more than 4% in about ten minutes and dropped below €2,000, while bitcoin slid to about $73,760, its lowest level since August, according to CoinDesk and CryptoTicker. More than $1 billion in leveraged crypto bets were forcibly closed over 24 hours. Both coins bounced a little overnight, but the drop came on top of a week in which investors pulled $1.28 billion out of US bitcoin ETFs.

## What happened

Crypto was the only big market open over the weekend, so it took the first hit from two pieces of news. CoinDesk points to an independent board review at Nvidia, following press reports about its chief executive, and to US Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying talks with Iran had made no progress. Nvidia said it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, CoinDesk reports.

The sharpest move came in ether. According to CryptoTicker, citing CoinGecko and Bitstamp data, ether held between $2,213 and $2,224 until 22:45 German time on Sunday, then fell to about $2,122 by 23:00. On Bitstamp, ETH/EUR touched a low of €1,858.11. CoinDesk says ether then recovered to around $2,144, and bitcoin climbed back above $75,000 after its low near $73,761.

The selling hit traders using borrowed money hardest. CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk shows more than $1 billion in positions liquidated in 24 hours, $913 million of them bets on rising prices. Ether accounted for $448 million and bitcoin for $272 million, and the single largest liquidation was a $48 million ether position. CryptoTicker notes that no exchange or authority has named a single trigger for the ten-minute drop; thin weekend trading and clusters of leveraged positions made it worse.

## Why it matters

This was not a one-off. Ether has lost about 14.6% in 30 days and sits roughly 57% below its August 2025 record of $4,946, according to CoinGecko figures cited by CryptoTicker.

Big investors have been leaving too. According to The Block, citing SoSoValue data, US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $1.28 billion in the week to 6 November, their worst week since late June, which turned their net flows for 2026 negative at about −$914 million. US spot ether ETFs lost $365 million, their sixth negative week in a row, with about $2 billion withdrawn since late September.

Sentiment is weak. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index stood at 29 on Monday, in "fear" territory, according to KuCoin's daily report. CoinDesk notes that bitcoin is sitting between its 200-day average, around $73,150, and its 100-day average, around $77,430. Those are levels traders watch, not forecasts.

## What this means for you

- **Leverage is what turned a dip into a wipe-out.** A 10x long is closed out after a drop of just under 10%, as CryptoTicker points out. If you don't understand liquidation prices, don't trade with borrowed money. Our [glossary](/glossary) explains the terms.
- **Weekend prices can move fast.** Fewer buyers and sellers means bigger swings on small news. If you buy or sell on a Sunday night, use limit orders rather than market orders.
- **Watch the euro price, not only the dollar one.** For EU readers the move below €2,000 is the one that shows up in your account. Exchange rates change the picture.
- **Use a licensed platform.** Under MiCA, check that your exchange is authorised in the EU before you move money. See [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained).
- **Taxes differ by country.** Selling at a loss can have tax consequences that depend on how long you've held the coins. Check your own country's rules before acting.

## What to watch

- **Monday's US stock open.** CoinDesk says whether the bounce holds depends largely on how Wall Street reacts to the Nvidia news and the Iran talks. A tokenized version of Nvidia stock was already trading about 3.6% lower over the weekend.
- **US inflation on Tuesday.** October consumer prices are due on 10 November. A hot number could add to the odds of a December Fed rate rise that markets are already pricing.
- **ETF flows.** Another week of outflows would show that large investors are still cutting exposure.
