**Short version:** A Bank of Russia rule taking effect today, 17 November, lets ordinary Russian investors buy bitcoin, ether and Tether's USDT through licensed brokers and exchanges for the first time. Non-professional buyers are limited to 300,000 rubles (about $3,700) a year per intermediary and must pass a test first. Nothing changes for people in the EU, where sanctions keep Russian crypto platforms off limits.

## What happened

Russia passed a law on digital currencies in July, and it took effect on 1 September. The Bank of Russia then wrote the detailed rules. The Justice Ministry registered them on 30 October, and the central bank confirmed on 6 November that they would apply from 17 November, according to Blockonomi.

Under the rules, according to CoinMarketCap Academy:

- **Non-qualified (ordinary) investors** can buy only bitcoin (BTC), ether (ETH) and USDT. They can spend up to 300,000 rubles a year through each intermediary.
- **Qualified investors**, who meet thresholds for assets, trading experience or financial qualifications, can buy any crypto-asset with no cap.
- **Everyone** must pass a test and read a risk warning before trading.
- Trades go through brokers, trust managers and licensed crypto exchange businesses, on exchange or over the counter.

The central bank picked the three coins by market value, daily trading volume and at least five years of price history on foreign exchanges. It says the aim is to limit ordinary investors' exposure to "the riskiest parts of the crypto market".

Today's date allows trading; it does not mean every venue opens today. From 22 November, Russian exchanges can start organised trading once the central bank has approved their trading rules, CoinDesk reports. The Moscow Exchange plans a pilot from 1 December, with trading from 6:50 to 23:50 Moscow time, Blockonomi reports, and Sberbank, Russia's largest bank, plans BTC, ETH and USDT products from the same date, according to CoinMarketCap Academy.

Some limits stay. Paying for goods and services in Russia with crypto remains banned. The law rules out moving coins from regulated accounts to your own wallet, and direct peer-to-peer deals are due to end in July 2027, CoinMarketCap Academy reports.

## Why it matters

Russia has gone from tolerating crypto in a grey zone to pulling it into its own regulated system. Russians have used crypto heavily for years, largely through peer-to-peer trades and foreign platforms. The new rules push that activity onto domestic venues the state can see, with a closed loop: no withdrawals to private wallets and an end date for peer-to-peer deals.

The caps are small: about $3,700 a year per intermediary for ordinary buyers. The bigger change is structural: a G20 central bank has picked a short list of "approved" crypto-assets for the public, much as some regulators approve a list of funds.

## What this means for you

- **If you live in the EU, nothing changes.** You cannot use these Russian venues. Since January 2024, Russian nationals and residents may not own or run EU firms offering crypto wallet, account or custody services, and the EU's 20th sanctions package banned dealings with crypto service providers based in Russia or Belarus, Elliptic reports.
- **EU firms face extra risk if they get this wrong.** The 21st package, adopted in July, lets the EU ban dealings with crypto platforms in any third country that keeps helping Russia evade sanctions, according to Elliptic. If an exchange you use offers ruble trading or Russian-linked tokens, that is a warning sign.
- **Compare the approaches.** The EU regulates the companies under MiCA rather than capping how much each person can buy. See [MiCA explained](/learn/mica-explained) and [choosing an exchange under MiCA](/school/exchanges-and-mica).
- **Expect scams to use the news.** "Now legal in Russia" or "approved by the central bank" are easy hooks for fake platforms. A real licence can be checked on the regulator's own register.

## What to watch

- Whether exchanges get their trading rules approved in time for 22 November, and whether the Moscow Exchange and Sberbank launch on 1 December.
- How much demand the 300,000-ruble cap leaves, and whether Russia raises it.
- Whether the EU names any country under its new third-country crypto ban.
