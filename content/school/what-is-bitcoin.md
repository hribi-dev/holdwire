## The short version

**Bitcoin** is digital money that runs on its own blockchain. It was described in 2008 in a paper by someone using the name **Satoshi Nakamoto**, whose real identity is still unknown. The network started in January 2009.

No company, bank or government runs it. Thousands of nodes check the rules, and miners add new blocks using proof of work.

## Where new bitcoin come from

New bitcoin are created as a reward for the miner who adds each block. A new block arrives roughly **every 10 minutes**.

Every **210,000 blocks**, about every four years, that reward is cut in half. This is the **halving**. The reward started at 50 bitcoin per block and has halved four times so far, most recently in April 2024, to 3.125 bitcoin. The next halving is expected around 2028.

Because the reward keeps halving, the total supply creeps towards a hard cap of **21 million** bitcoin, which it will reach around the year 2140.

## "Digital gold"?

Fans call Bitcoin digital gold: scarce, hard to seize, and outside any government's control. Some see it as a hedge against inflation.

Worth knowing before you believe that:

- Bitcoin's price has **fallen more than 70% several times**, including in 2018 and 2022.
- It often moves together with tech shares when markets get nervous, rather than acting like a safe haven.
- A fixed supply limits how many coins exist. It says nothing about how much people will pay for them.

## Small units

You don't have to buy a whole bitcoin. One bitcoin is divided into 100 million **satoshis** ("sats"), so you can buy a few euros' worth.
