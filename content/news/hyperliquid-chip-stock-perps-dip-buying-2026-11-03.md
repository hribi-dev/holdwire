**Twenty-six large Hyperliquid accounts added about $43.8 million in net long positions on stock-linked perpetual contracts as chip shares sold off, TokenPost reported on 3 November.**

## What happened

The buying spread across 18 stock-linked markets, with nearly 40% going into three storage and chip names, according to monitoring data from TradingBeats reported by MarsBit. The data covered 145 stock contracts run by 10 HIP-3 deployers, the outside teams that build their own markets on Hyperliquid. SOXL, a 3x leveraged chip-stock fund, fell nearly 15% in 24 hours, and several chip and storage contracts dropped close to 9%. On Monday the Nasdaq-100 closed more than 10% below its peak, according to Ted Pillows' daily market report.

It was not all one-way. The same monitoring counted about $30.4 million of liquidations in the prior 24 hours across at least 11 accounts, each over $1 million. About 85% were long positions, including about $6.1 million in HYPE longs. HIP-3 markets had their own losses: a crude oil long and a pair of chip-stock longs were liquidated for about $1.5 million and $1.3 million.

## What it means for EU readers

Stock perps on Hyperliquid trade around the clock, with leverage, and keep moving when stock exchanges are shut. That draws traders in during sell-offs, but leveraged positions can be wiped out quickly, as last night's liquidations show. These contracts are not regulated EU investment products, and a perp is not the same as owning the share.
