**Binance's record $6.3 billion theft started four months earlier, with an engineer downloading a trojanised Homebrew installer in June, according to the exchange's first investigation report, published on 3 November.**

## What happened

Binance describes a multi-stage campaign, not a single breach, The Hacker News reports. In June, a wallet engineer looking for an AI tool installed a fake version of Homebrew, a popular developer tool. Binance caught it and changed credentials, but details about the engineer's role and colleagues had already leaked.

In September, someone posing as a project director recruited a second engineer through a job platform. After video interviews with a "CEO" of a company that does not exist, the engineer was asked to download a coding assignment. It installed malware. On 20 October, the attacker used that computer to misuse an internal AI tool and reach other wallet engineers' machines. On 29 October, between 17:15 and 19:56, the attacker deployed a container and used the wallet-signing service to send funds outside the normal withdrawal process.

The FBI has attributed the theft to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, Unchained reports. About $2.85 billion has been frozen or intercepted, and 98% of affected token balances have been replaced, according to KuCoin's daily report.

## What it means for you

Binance says it is covering customer losses from its own funds, but the method is a warning for everyone. Fake job offers and "coding tests" are a common way North Korean hackers get in. Never run files or repositories sent by a recruiter on a device that holds keys or work access.
